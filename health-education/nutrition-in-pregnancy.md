<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📖 Introduction</h2>
<p>Pregnancy is a special period in a woman’s life when the nutritional needs of the mother increase because the baby grows inside the uterus. Proper nutrition during pregnancy helps in healthy growth of the fetus, prevents anemia, supports maternal health, improves immunity, reduces pregnancy complications, and prepares the mother for safe delivery and breastfeeding.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">🎯 General Objective</h2>
<p>At the end of the health education, antenatal mothers and family members will be able to understand the importance of proper nutrition during pregnancy and will be motivated to take a balanced diet, iron-folic acid tablets, calcium, adequate fluids, and healthy foods for the well-being of mother and baby.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Meaning of Nutrition in Pregnancy</h3>
<p>Nutrition in pregnancy means taking adequate and balanced food containing all essential nutrients required for the health of the mother and proper growth and development of the fetus. It includes carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, water, and protective foods in proper amounts.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Importance of Nutrition During Pregnancy</h3>
<ul>
<li>Promotes healthy growth and development of the fetus.</li>
<li>Maintains good health and strength of the mother.</li>
<li>Prevents anemia, weakness, low birth weight and malnutrition.</li>
<li>Supports development of placenta and amniotic fluid.</li>
<li>Helps in proper weight gain during pregnancy.</li>
<li>Improves immunity and reduces infection risk.</li>
<li>Prepares mother for safe delivery and breastfeeding.</li>
<li>Reduces risk of pregnancy complications.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Balanced Diet During Pregnancy</h3>
<p>A pregnant woman should eat a variety of foods from all food groups. She should not eat for two people in quantity, but she should eat better quality food and take extra nutritious food as advised.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Iron-Rich Foods and Anemia Prevention</h3>
<p>Anemia is common during pregnancy and can cause weakness, tiredness, breathlessness, low birth weight baby, premature delivery and complications during childbirth. Pregnant women should take iron-rich foods and Iron Folic Acid tablets as advised by health workers.</p>
<ul>
<li>Eat green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery, dates, groundnuts, sesame seeds, eggs, meat and fish.</li>
<li>Take vitamin C-rich foods like lemon, amla, guava and orange with meals to improve iron absorption.</li>
<li>Avoid tea or coffee immediately with meals because it reduces iron absorption.</li>
<li>Take IFA tablets regularly as advised during ANC visits.</li>
<li>Take deworming medicine only as advised by health worker.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Calcium and Bone Health</h3>
<p>Calcium is needed for development of baby’s bones and teeth and for maintaining mother’s bone strength. Calcium also supports muscle and nerve function.</p>
<ul>
<li>Take milk, curd, paneer, ragi, sesame seeds, green leafy vegetables and calcium-rich foods.</li>
<li>Take calcium tablets as advised by doctor or ANM.</li>
<li>Do not take calcium and iron tablets together at the same time; keep a gap as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Foods to Include During Pregnancy</h3>
<ul>
<li>Home-cooked fresh food.</li>
<li>Rice, roti, dal, vegetables, curd, milk, fruits and salads.</li>
<li>Seasonal fruits and vegetables.</li>
<li>Protein foods like dal, chana, soybean, egg, paneer, fish or meat as per food habit.</li>
<li>Iron-rich and calcium-rich foods daily.</li>
<li>Clean drinking water and healthy fluids.</li>
<li>Small frequent meals if nausea or heartburn is present.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Foods and Habits to Avoid During Pregnancy</h3>
<ul>
<li>Avoid alcohol, tobacco, smoking, gutkha and drugs.</li>
<li>Avoid stale, uncovered and contaminated food.</li>
<li>Avoid excessive fried food, spicy food, junk food and packaged snacks.</li>
<li>Avoid unwashed fruits and vegetables.</li>
<li>Avoid self-medication and unnecessary supplements without advice.</li>
<li>Limit tea and coffee, especially around meal time.</li>
<li>Avoid unsafe or unpasteurized milk products.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. Hydration During Pregnancy</h3>
<ul>
<li>Drink adequate clean and safe water daily.</li>
<li>Take fluids like buttermilk, lemon water, coconut water or soup according to availability.</li>
<li>Increase fluids in hot weather, constipation or mild nausea.</li>
<li>Seek medical advice if there is severe vomiting, dehydration or inability to drink.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Important Dietary Advice for Pregnant Women</h3>
<ul>
<li>Do not skip meals, especially breakfast.</li>
<li>Eat one extra nutritious meal during pregnancy as advised.</li>
<li>Take IFA and calcium tablets regularly as per health worker’s advice.</li>
<li>Use iodized salt in daily food.</li>
<li>Maintain hygiene while cooking and eating.</li>
<li>Wash hands before preparing and eating food.</li>
<li>Wash fruits and vegetables properly before eating.</li>
<li>Attend regular antenatal check-ups for weight, hemoglobin and blood pressure monitoring.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">12. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA</h3>
<ul>
<li>Assess nutritional status and dietary habits of pregnant women.</li>
<li>Provide counselling regarding balanced diet and extra food.</li>
<li>Educate about IFA, calcium, deworming and tetanus/diphtheria immunization as per guidelines.</li>
<li>Identify anemia, undernutrition, excessive vomiting and high-risk pregnancy.</li>
<li>Encourage regular ANC visits and institutional delivery.</li>
<li>Educate family members to support mother with nutritious food and rest.</li>
<li>Maintain records in MCP card and provide follow-up.</li>
</ul>

<div style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:20px;border-radius:10px;margin:15px 0;">
<ol>
<li>Nutrition during pregnancy is important for mother and baby.</li>
<li>A pregnant woman should take a balanced diet with all food groups.</li>
<li>Protein, iron, folic acid, calcium, iodine, vitamins and fluids are very important.</li>
<li>Iron-rich foods and IFA tablets help prevent anemia.</li>
<li>Calcium-rich foods and calcium tablets support bone development.</li>
<li>Alcohol, tobacco, stale food, junk food and self-medication should be avoided.</li>
<li>Regular ANC check-up and nutrition counselling are necessary.</li>

<p>Nutrition in pregnancy is essential for safe motherhood and healthy fetal development.
