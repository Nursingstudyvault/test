<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📖 Introduction</h2>
<p>Snake bite is a medical emergency. In rural and agricultural areas, snake bites commonly occur while working in fields, walking at night, sleeping on the floor, or handling firewood and stones. Some snake bites may be poisonous and can cause serious complications such as bleeding, paralysis, breathing difficulty, shock, kidney failure, and even death. Correct first aid and early transport to hospital can save life.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Meaning of Snake Bite</h3>
<p>Snake bite is an injury caused by the bite of a snake. Some snakes are non-poisonous, while some are poisonous and inject venom into the body. Snake venom can affect blood clotting, nervous system, muscles, heart, kidneys and breathing. Every snake bite should be treated as an emergency until examined by a health professional.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Common Places and Situations of Snake Bite</h3>
<ul>
<li>Working in agricultural fields.</li>
<li>Walking barefoot at night.</li>
<li>Sleeping on the floor without protection.</li>
<li>Putting hands inside holes, bushes, stones or wood piles.</li>
<li>Collecting firewood or grass.</li>
<li>Living near farms, forests, water bodies or garbage areas.</li>
<li>During rainy season when snakes come out of burrows.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Signs and Symptoms of Snake Bite</h3>

<h4>Local Symptoms:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Fang marks or bite marks.</li>
<li>Pain at bite site.</li>
<li>Swelling around bite area.</li>
<li>Redness, warmth or tenderness.</li>
<li>Bleeding or oozing from bite site.</li>
<li>Blisters or discoloration in severe cases.</li>
</ul>

<h4>General Symptoms of Poisonous Snake Bite:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Nausea and vomiting.</li>
<li>Dizziness or weakness.</li>
<li>Sweating and anxiety.</li>
<li>Difficulty in breathing.</li>
<li>Drooping eyelids, blurred vision or difficulty speaking.</li>
<li>Bleeding from gums, nose, urine or stool.</li>
<li>Severe abdominal pain.</li>
<li>Dark urine or reduced urine output.</li>
<li>Unconsciousness or shock in severe cases.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Immediate First Aid for Snake Bite</h3>

<div style="background:#d4edda;border:1px solid #28a745;padding:15px;border-radius:8px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>✅ Correct First Aid Steps:</strong>
<ol>
<li><strong>Stay calm and reassure the patient:</strong> Panic increases heart rate and may spread venom faster.</li>
<li><strong>Move the patient away from the snake:</strong> Prevent another bite.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the patient at rest:</strong> Avoid walking, running or unnecessary movement.</li>
<li><strong>Immobilize the bitten limb:</strong> Keep the limb still using a splint or support.</li>
<li><strong>Keep the bitten part below heart level if possible:</strong> This may slow venom spread.</li>
<li><strong>Remove tight items:</strong> Remove rings, bangles, anklets, shoes or tight clothes before swelling starts.</li>
<li><strong>Cover the wound lightly:</strong> Use clean cloth or sterile dressing if available.</li>
<li><strong>Arrange immediate transport:</strong> Take the patient to the nearest hospital or health facility quickly.</li>
<li><strong>Note the time of bite:</strong> Tell health staff when the bite occurred.</li>
<li><strong>Observe symptoms:</strong> Breathing difficulty, swelling, bleeding, weakness or unconsciousness should be reported.</li>
</ol>
</div>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Immobilization of Bitten Limb</h3>
<p>Immobilization means keeping the bitten limb still to reduce movement and slow spread of venom.</p>
<ul>
<li>Ask the patient not to move the bitten limb.</li>
<li>Use a wooden stick, cardboard, umbrella, folded newspaper or any firm support as a splint.</li>
<li>Apply bandage gently to support the limb, but do not tie tightly.</li>
<li>Check that fingers or toes remain warm and pink.</li>
<li>Do not block blood circulation.</li>
<li>Carry the patient instead of allowing walking if possible.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. What Not to Do in Snake Bite</h3>

<div style="background:#f8d7da;border:1px solid #dc3545;padding:15px;border-radius:8px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>❌ Avoid Harmful Practices:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Do not cut the wound with blade or knife.</li>
<li>Do not suck the venom by mouth.</li>
<li>Do not apply ice directly on the bite.</li>
<li>Do not apply tight tourniquet.</li>
<li>Do not burn the wound.</li>
<li>Do not apply mud, herbal paste, cow dung, chemicals or traditional medicines on wound.</li>
<li>Do not give alcohol to the patient.</li>
<li>Do not make the patient run or walk unnecessarily.</li>
<li>Do not waste time in faith healing or local remedies.</li>
<li>Do not try to catch or kill the snake if it is risky.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Importance of Early Hospital Referral</h3>
<p>Snake bite needs urgent medical assessment. Anti-snake venom is the specific treatment for poisonous snake bite and is available at health facilities. Early treatment reduces complications and saves life.</p>
<ul>
<li>Take the patient to hospital as early as possible.</li>
<li>Inform health staff about time and site of bite.</li>
<li>If possible, describe the snake from a safe distance, but do not risk another bite.</li>
<li>Hospital staff may monitor breathing, blood pressure, bleeding, urine output and swelling.</li>
<li>Anti-snake venom should be given only by trained health professionals.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. Danger Signs After Snake Bite</h3>
<ul>
<li>Difficulty in breathing.</li>
<li>Drooping eyelids or difficulty opening eyes.</li>
<li>Difficulty speaking or swallowing.</li>
<li>Bleeding from gums, nose, urine, stool or wound.</li>
<li>Rapidly increasing swelling.</li>
<li>Severe pain or blackening of skin.</li>
<li>Persistent vomiting.</li>
<li>Very low blood pressure, fainting or shock.</li>
<li>Reduced urine output or dark urine.</li>
<li>Unconsciousness or convulsions.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Prevention of Snake Bite</h3>
<ul>
<li>Wear shoes or boots while walking outside, especially at night.</li>
<li>Use torch or flashlight while walking in dark areas.</li>
<li>Avoid putting hands into holes, bushes, stones or wood piles.</li>
<li>Use a stick to move grass or bushes before stepping.</li>
<li>Keep house and surroundings clean.</li>
<li>Remove garbage, stones, wood piles and rodent hiding places near home.</li>
<li>Store grains and food properly to reduce rats, because rats attract snakes.</li>
<li>Sleep on cot or bed instead of floor when possible.</li>
<li>Use mosquito net or bed net tucked properly while sleeping.</li>
<li>Teach children not to play with snakes.</li>
<li>Do not try to catch, tease or kill snakes.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA</h3>
<ul>
<li>Educate community about snake bite prevention.</li>
<li>Teach correct first aid and harmful practices to avoid.</li>
<li>Encourage early referral to hospital.</li>
<li>Demonstrate limb immobilization.</li>
<li>Identify danger signs and arrange emergency transport.</li>
<li>Promote use of footwear, torch and safe sleeping practices.</li>
<li>Maintain records of snake bite cases in the community.</li>
<li>Provide follow-up education after recovery.</li>
</ul>

<li>Remove tight items like rings, bangles and shoes.</li>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">🏁 Conclusion</h2>
<p>Snake bite is a serious emergency that requires correct first aid and immediate hospital referral. The patient should be kept calm, the bitten limb should be immobilized, and harmful practices like cutting, sucking, burning, tight tourniquet and herbal application should be avoided. Early treatment with anti-snake venom and supportive care can save life. Community awareness and preventive measures are essential to reduce snake bite incidents.</p>
