<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📖 Introduction</h2>
<p>
Kangaroo Mother Care, also called KMC, is a simple, safe and effective method of caring for low birth weight and preterm babies. In KMC, the baby is kept in direct skin-to-skin contact on the mother’s chest. It helps to keep the baby warm, supports breastfeeding, improves weight gain, reduces infection risk and strengthens bonding between mother and baby. KMC can be given by mother, father or another trained family member.
</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">🎯 General Objective</h2>
<p>
At the end of the health education, postnatal mothers and family members will be able to understand the meaning, importance, benefits, correct method and precautions of Kangaroo Mother Care and will be motivated to practice KMC for low birth weight and preterm babies as advised by health workers.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Meaning of Kangaroo Mother Care</h3>
<p>
Kangaroo Mother Care is a special method of newborn care in which the baby is kept in direct skin-to-skin contact on the mother’s bare chest. The baby is placed upright between the mother’s breasts and covered properly to maintain warmth. KMC is especially useful for low birth weight and preterm babies.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Need and Importance of KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Low birth weight and preterm babies lose body heat quickly.</li>
<li>KMC helps to maintain the baby’s body temperature naturally.</li>
<li>It supports early and exclusive breastfeeding.</li>
<li>It improves baby’s weight gain and growth.</li>
<li>It reduces the risk of infection and hypothermia.</li>
<li>It increases bonding between mother and baby.</li>
<li>It gives confidence to mother and family members in newborn care.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Babies Who Need KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Low birth weight babies.</li>
<li>Preterm babies.</li>
<li>Small babies who are clinically stable.</li>
<li>Babies who have difficulty maintaining body temperature.</li>
<li>Babies discharged from SNCU/NICU who need continued warmth and breastfeeding support.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Components of Kangaroo Mother Care</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Skin-to-skin contact:</strong> Baby is placed on mother’s bare chest in upright position.</li>
<li><strong>Exclusive breastfeeding:</strong> Baby is breastfed frequently according to feeding cues.</li>
<li><strong>Early discharge with follow-up:</strong> Stable babies may continue KMC at home with regular follow-up.</li>
<li><strong>Family support:</strong> Mother, father or trained family member can provide KMC.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Preparation Before Giving KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Wash hands properly with soap and water.</li>
<li>Mother should wear front-open clean clothes.</li>
<li>Remove jewelry or sharp objects that may hurt the baby.</li>
<li>Ensure privacy and comfortable sitting or semi-reclining position.</li>
<li>Baby should wear cap, socks and diaper/nappy.</li>
<li>Keep baby’s chest bare for skin-to-skin contact.</li>
<li>Use clean cloth binder, dupatta or KMC wrap to support the baby safely.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Steps of Kangaroo Mother Care</h3>
<ol>
<li>Mother should sit comfortably in a chair or bed with back support.</li>
<li>Wash hands before touching the baby.</li>
<li>Place the baby upright between the mother’s breasts.</li>
<li>Keep the baby’s chest in direct contact with mother’s chest.</li>
<li>Turn baby’s head to one side and keep the neck slightly extended to maintain airway.</li>
<li>Keep baby’s hips flexed and abducted in frog-like position.</li>
<li>Cover the baby’s back with clean cloth or KMC binder.</li>
<li>Cover baby’s head with cap and feet with socks.</li>
<li>Ensure the baby is breathing comfortably and face is visible.</li>
<li>Breastfeed the baby frequently during KMC.</li>
<li>Continue KMC for as long as possible, preferably several hours daily as advised.</li>
</ol>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Correct Position of Baby During KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Baby should be placed upright on mother’s chest.</li>
<li>Baby’s head should be turned to one side.</li>
<li>Neck should be slightly extended, not bent.</li>
<li>Baby’s nose and mouth should not be covered.</li>
<li>Baby’s abdomen should be at mother’s upper abdomen level.</li>
<li>Baby’s legs should be flexed like frog position.</li>
<li>Baby should be firmly supported with cloth binder but not too tight.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. Duration of KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>KMC should be started as early as possible when the baby is clinically stable.</li>
<li>Each KMC session should be continued for at least 1 hour to avoid frequent handling.</li>
<li>Longer duration is better for warmth, breastfeeding and bonding.</li>
<li>KMC can be continued day and night with breaks for mother’s comfort.</li>
<li>Father or another trained family member can provide KMC when mother needs rest.</li>
<li>KMC should be continued until the baby maintains temperature, gains weight and no longer accepts KMC position comfortably.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Breastfeeding During KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>KMC helps the baby remain close to the breast.</li>
<li>It improves early initiation and continuation of breastfeeding.</li>
<li>Mother should breastfeed frequently according to baby’s demand.</li>
<li>Exclusive breastfeeding should be continued for the first 6 months.</li>
<li>If the baby is unable to suck properly, expressed breast milk may be given as advised by health worker.</li>
<li>Mother should take adequate diet, fluids and rest to support milk production.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Precautions During KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Always keep baby’s airway clear.</li>
<li>Baby’s face, nose and mouth should remain visible.</li>
<li>Do not tie the cloth binder too tightly.</li>
<li>Maintain hand hygiene before handling baby.</li>
<li>Keep baby warm with cap, socks and clean cloth.</li>
<li>Do not give KMC if mother has severe illness without medical advice.</li>
<li>Do not allow people with cough, cold or infection to handle the baby.</li>
<li>Observe baby’s breathing, color, activity and feeding.</li>
<li>Seek medical help if any danger sign appears.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">12. Danger Signs in Baby</h3>
<ul>
<li>Difficulty in breathing or fast breathing</li>
<li>Chest indrawing</li>
<li>Bluish discoloration of lips or body</li>
<li>Baby feels cold or too hot</li>
<li>Poor feeding or unable to suck</li>
<li>Repeated vomiting</li>
<li>Convulsions</li>
<li>Lethargy or unconsciousness</li>
<li>Fever</li>
<li>Yellow discoloration of skin or eyes</li>
<li>Umbilical redness, pus or foul smell</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">13. Home Care Advice for KMC</h3>
<ul>
<li>Continue KMC at home as advised by doctor or nurse.</li>
<li>Keep the room warm and clean.</li>
<li>Wash hands before touching the baby.</li>
<li>Breastfeed frequently and exclusively.</li>
<li>Do not bathe low birth weight baby frequently; follow health worker’s advice.</li>
<li>Keep baby away from sick persons and crowded places.</li>
<li>Attend regular follow-up visits for weight monitoring.</li>
<li>Maintain immunization schedule as per age.</li>
<li>Observe baby for danger signs daily.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">14. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA</h3>
<ul>
<li>Identify low birth weight and preterm babies who need KMC.</li>
<li>Explain the importance and benefits of KMC to mother and family.</li>
<li>Demonstrate correct KMC position using baby doll or actual baby under supervision.</li>
<li>Teach mother about breastfeeding during KMC.</li>
<li>Observe baby’s temperature, breathing, color and feeding.</li>
<li>Encourage father and family members to support mother.</li>
<li>Teach hygiene, precautions and danger signs.</li>
<li>Provide follow-up for weight gain, feeding and newborn health.</li>
<li>Maintain records and refer baby if danger signs are present.</li>
</ul>

<div style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:20px;border-radius:10px;margin:15px 0;">
<ol>
<li>Kangaroo Mother Care is skin-to-skin contact between mother and baby.</li>
<li>It is very useful for low birth weight and preterm babies.</li>
<li>KMC helps maintain warmth, improves breastfeeding and supports weight gain.</li>
<li>Baby should be placed upright between mother’s breasts with head turned to one side.</li>
<li>Baby’s airway should always remain clear.</li>
<li>KMC can be continued at home with proper guidance and follow-up.</li>
<li>Danger signs such as poor feeding, breathing difficulty, fever, cold body or convulsions need immediate medical help.</li>
</ol>
</div>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">🏁 Conclusion</h2>
<p>
Kangaroo Mother Care is a simple, low-cost and effective method for caring for low birth weight and preterm babies. It provides warmth, supports breastfeeding, improves weight gain, reduces infection risk and strengthens mother-baby bonding. With proper guidance, hygiene and family support, KMC can be safely continued at home and helps improve newborn survival.
