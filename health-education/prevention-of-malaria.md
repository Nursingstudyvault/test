<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📖 Introduction</h2>
<p>
Malaria is a common mosquito-borne infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasite. It spreads through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquito. Malaria is common in areas where stagnant water is present and mosquito breeding is high. It can affect children, adults, pregnant women and elderly people. Malaria can be prevented by controlling mosquito breeding, protecting ourselves from mosquito bites, maintaining environmental cleanliness and taking early diagnosis and treatment.
</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">🎯 General Objective</h2>
<p>
At the end of the health education, community people and family members will be able to understand the causes, transmission, signs and symptoms, prevention and control measures of malaria and will be motivated to prevent mosquito breeding, protect themselves from mosquito bites and seek early treatment.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Meaning of Malaria</h3>
<p>
Malaria is a mosquito-borne infectious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites. It spreads to humans through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquito. Malaria mainly affects red blood cells and causes fever, chills, sweating, headache, weakness and body ache.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Cause of Malaria</h3>
<ul>
<li>Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasite.</li>
<li>The parasite is transmitted by infected female Anopheles mosquito.</li>
<li>Mosquitoes breed in stagnant and dirty water.</li>
<li>Common mosquito breeding places are coolers, water tanks, flower pots, tyres, drains, pits, roof gutters, broken containers and open water storage vessels.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Mode of Transmission</h3>
<ul>
<li>Malaria spreads through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquito.</li>
<li>When a mosquito bites a malaria patient, it takes malaria parasite with blood.</li>
<li>When the same infected mosquito bites a healthy person, the parasite enters the healthy person’s body.</li>
<li>Malaria does not spread by touching, sharing food, sitting near the patient or using the same utensils.</li>
<li>Rarely, malaria may spread through infected blood transfusion or from mother to baby, but mosquito bite is the common mode of transmission.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Signs and Symptoms of Malaria</h3>
<ul>
<li>High fever with chills and shivering</li>
<li>Excessive sweating after fever</li>
<li>Headache</li>
<li>Body ache and joint pain</li>
<li>Nausea and vomiting</li>
<li>Weakness and tiredness</li>
<li>Loss of appetite</li>
<li>Pale skin due to anemia in repeated infection</li>
<li>Enlarged spleen in some cases</li>
<li>In severe malaria: confusion, convulsions, unconsciousness, jaundice, breathing difficulty or reduced urine output</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. High-Risk Groups</h3>
<ul>
<li>Children below 5 years</li>
<li>Pregnant women</li>
<li>Elderly people</li>
<li>People with low immunity</li>
<li>People living near stagnant water areas</li>
<li>People sleeping without mosquito nets</li>
<li>Outdoor workers and night-shift workers</li>
<li>People living in malaria-endemic areas</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Prevention of Malaria</h3>

<h4 style="color:#1557b0;">A. Prevent Mosquito Breeding</h4>
<ul>
<li>Do not allow water to collect around the house.</li>
<li>Empty and scrub coolers, buckets, pots and water containers every week.</li>
<li>Cover water storage tanks, drums and vessels properly.</li>
<li>Fill pits and low areas where rainwater collects.</li>
<li>Remove broken pots, coconut shells, tyres, plastic cups and waste materials that collect water.</li>
<li>Keep drains clean and flowing.</li>
<li>Change water in flower pots and animal drinking containers regularly.</li>
<li>Keep roof gutters clean.</li>
<li>Dispose garbage properly.</li>
<li>Use larvivorous fish in ponds where suitable and advised by health authorities.</li>
</ul>

<h4 style="color:#1557b0;">B. Protection from Mosquito Bites</h4>
<ul>
<li>Use mosquito net while sleeping, especially for children and pregnant women.</li>
<li>Use insecticide-treated bed nets if available.</li>
<li>Wear full-sleeved clothes and long pants in evening and night.</li>
<li>Use mosquito repellents, coils or vaporizers safely.</li>
<li>Install wire mesh on doors and windows.</li>
<li>Close doors and windows during evening time.</li>
<li>Avoid sleeping outside without mosquito protection.</li>
<li>Use curtains or screens to reduce mosquito entry.</li>
</ul>

<h4 style="color:#1557b0;">C. Environmental Cleanliness</h4>
<ul>
<li>Keep house and surroundings clean.</li>
<li>Do not throw garbage in drains.</li>
<li>Maintain proper drainage system.</li>
<li>Keep grass and bushes around the house trimmed.</li>
<li>Participate in community cleanliness drives.</li>
<li>Inform local health workers about stagnant water areas.</li>
</ul>

<h4 style="color:#1557b0;">D. Early Diagnosis and Treatment</h4>
<ul>
<li>Do not ignore fever with chills and sweating.</li>
<li>Visit the nearest health centre for blood test if fever occurs.</li>
<li>Take anti-malarial medicines only as prescribed by doctor or health worker.</li>
<li>Complete the full course of treatment even if symptoms improve.</li>
<li>Do not take incomplete or leftover medicines.</li>
<li>Avoid self-medication.</li>
<li>Follow the advice of health worker for repeat testing or follow-up.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Home Care for Malaria Patient</h3>
<ul>
<li>Give adequate rest to the patient.</li>
<li>Give plenty of fluids such as water, ORS, coconut water, lemon water and soup if allowed.</li>
<li>Give light and nutritious food.</li>
<li>Give medicines exactly as prescribed.</li>
<li>Monitor fever and general condition.</li>
<li>Use mosquito net for the patient to prevent mosquitoes from biting the patient and spreading malaria to others.</li>
<li>Do not allow mosquitoes to bite the patient.</li>
<li>Take the patient to hospital immediately if condition worsens.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. Danger Signs Requiring Immediate Hospital Visit</h3>
<ul>
<li>Very high fever not reducing</li>
<li>Repeated vomiting</li>
<li>Convulsions</li>
<li>Unconsciousness or confusion</li>
<li>Severe weakness</li>
<li>Difficulty in breathing</li>
<li>Yellow eyes or jaundice</li>
<li>Dark urine or reduced urine output</li>
<li>Severe anemia or extreme paleness</li>
<li>Malaria symptoms in pregnant woman or small child</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA</h3>
<ul>
<li>Educate families about malaria prevention and mosquito control.</li>
<li>Identify fever cases in the community.</li>
<li>Encourage blood testing for suspected malaria.</li>
<li>Guide patients to complete treatment.</li>
<li>Demonstrate correct use of mosquito nets.</li>
<li>Promote environmental sanitation and source reduction.</li>
<li>Coordinate with health team for fogging and anti-larval activities.</li>
<li>Provide special guidance to pregnant women, children and elderly people.</li>
<li>Maintain records and report malaria cases as per protocol.</li>
<li>Encourage community participation in malaria control activities.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">✅ Summary</h2>

<div style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:20px;border-radius:10px;margin:15px 0;">
<ol>
<li>Malaria is caused by Plasmodium parasite.</li>
<li>It spreads through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquito.</li>
<li>Common symptoms are fever with chills, sweating, headache, vomiting and weakness.</li>
<li>Mosquitoes breed in stagnant water.</li>
<li>Malaria can be prevented by removing stagnant water and using mosquito protection.</li>
<li>Fever with chills should be tested early at a health centre.</li>
<li>Anti-malarial medicines should be taken only as prescribed and full course should be completed.</li>
</ol>
</div>

🌟 <strong>Stop mosquito breeding, prevent mosquito bites and take early treatment — malaria can be prevented.</strong>
</blockquote>

<p>
Malaria is a preventable and treatable disease. Active participation of family members, community people and health workers is essential for malaria prevention and control.
