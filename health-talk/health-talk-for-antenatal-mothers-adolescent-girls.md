<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Introduction (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Greeting:</strong> "Good morning, respected madam and my dear mothers and sisters! My name is [Your Name] and I am a nursing student from [College Name]. Today, I am going to talk to you about a very common but serious health problem that affects many women in our community — <strong>Anemia</strong>, which many of you know as 'khoon ki kami' (blood deficiency)."</p>
<p><strong>Ice-Breaking:</strong> "How many of you sometimes feel very tired, weak, or dizzy? Raise your hands please. These could be signs that your body needs more iron. Today we will learn all about this!"</p>

<blockquote style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Key Message:</strong> "Healthy blood makes a healthy body — prevent anemia, stay strong!"
</blockquote>

<!-- ==================== DEFINITION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. What is Anemia? (2-3 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Simple Definition:</strong> "Anemia means your blood has become weak — it does not have enough <strong>hemoglobin</strong>. Hemoglobin is the red substance in your blood that carries oxygen to all parts of your body. When hemoglobin is low, your body does not get enough oxygen, and you feel weak and tired."</p>
<p><strong>Scientific Definition:</strong> Anemia is defined as a condition in which the number of red blood cells (RBCs) or the hemoglobin concentration within them is <strong>below normal</strong> for the individual's age, sex, and physiological status. According to WHO, hemoglobin less than <strong>11 g/dL</strong> in pregnant women and less than <strong>12 g/dL</strong> in non-pregnant women is considered anemia.</p>

<div style="background:#d4edda;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #28a745;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Normal Hemoglobin Levels:</strong><br>
• Adult Non-Pregnant Women: 12-16 g/dL<br>
• Pregnant Women: 11-14 g/dL<br>
• Adolescent Girls (12-19 years): 12-15 g/dL
</div>

<!-- ==================== MAGNITUDE ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. How Common is Anemia? (2 Minutes)</h3>
<p>Anemia is a <strong>major public health problem</strong> in India:</p>
<ul>
<li>According to NFHS-5 (National Family Health Survey), <strong>57% of women</strong> in India are anemic</li>
<li><strong>52% of pregnant women</strong> suffer from anemia</li>
<li><strong>54% of adolescent girls</strong> are anemic</li>
<li>Anemia contributes to <strong>20-40% of maternal deaths</strong> in India</li>
</ul>

<div style="background:#f8d7da;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #dc3545;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>🚨 Anemia in pregnancy can lead to:</strong>
</div>

<div class="table-wrapper">
<table>
<thead>
<tr style="background:#dc3545;color:white;"><th>Complication</th><th>Explanation</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Maternal Death</strong></td><td>Severe anemia can cause heart failure during delivery</td></tr>
<tr style="background:#fce4e4;"><td><strong>Preterm Birth</strong></td><td>Baby born before 37 weeks — low birth weight</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Postpartum Hemorrhage</strong></td><td>Excessive bleeding after delivery; anemic women cannot tolerate even normal blood loss</td></tr>
<tr style="background:#fce4e4;"><td><strong>Low Birth Weight Baby</strong></td><td>Baby weighs less than 2.5 kg at birth</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Increased Infections</strong></td><td>Low immunity in both mother and baby</td></tr>

</tbody>

<!-- ==================== ENHANCE IRON ABSORPTION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Tips to Enhance Iron Absorption (3-4 Minutes)</h3>

<div style="background:#d4edda;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #28a745;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>✅ Do's (Increase Iron Absorption):</strong>
<ul style="margin:10px 0 0 20px;">
<li>Eat <strong>Vitamin C rich foods</strong> with meals — lemon, amla, orange, tomato, guava</li>
<li>Cook food in <strong>iron kadai (cast iron pan)</strong> — adds iron to food</li>
<li>Eat <strong>fermented foods</strong> — idli, dosa, dhokla (improves absorption)</li>
<li>Add <strong>sprouted grains and pulses</strong> to your diet</li>
<li>Eat <strong>jaggery (gud) after meals</strong> instead of sugar</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background:#f8d7da;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #dc3545;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>❌ Don'ts (Reduce Iron Absorption):</strong>
<ul style="margin:10px 0 0 20px;">
<li>Do NOT drink <strong>tea or coffee</strong> immediately after meals — wait at least 1 hour</li>
<li>Avoid <strong>calcium-rich foods (milk, paneer)</strong> at the same time as iron-rich meals</li>
<li>Avoid eating <strong>excessively spicy/oily food</strong> that irritates the stomach</li>
</ul>
</div>

<!-- ==================== IFA TABLETS ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. IFA (Iron Folic Acid) Tablets — Your Free Medicine (4-5 Minutes)</h3>

<div class="table-wrapper">
<table>
<thead>
<tr style="background:#1a73e8;color:white;"><th>Group</th><th>IFA Dosage</th><th>Duration</th></tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><strong>Pregnant Women</strong></td><td>1 tablet daily (100 mg Iron + 0.5 mg Folic Acid)</td><td>Throughout pregnancy & 6 months after delivery</td></tr>
<tr style="background:#e8f0fe;"><td><strong>Adolescent Girls (12-19 years)</strong></td><td>1 tablet weekly (Blue IFA)</td><td>Weekly throughout the year</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Lactating Mothers</strong></td><td>1 tablet daily</td><td>6 months after delivery</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>
</div>

<blockquote style="background:#fff3cd;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #ffc107;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>💊 Important Points About IFA Tablets:</strong><br>
• Available <strong>FREE</strong> at all Anganwadi Centres, PHCs, and Sub-Centres<br>
• Take IFA tablet <strong>at bedtime</strong> to reduce nausea<br>
• Take with <strong>lemon water</strong> (Vitamin C) for better absorption<br>
• <strong>Do NOT take with milk or tea</strong><br>
• Stools may become black — this is <strong>normal</strong>, do not worry<br>
• Store tablets <strong>away from children</strong> — overdose is dangerous
</blockquote>

<!-- ==================== PREVENTION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Prevention of Anemia — Simple Steps (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Eat iron-rich foods daily</strong> — green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery, dates</li>
<li><strong>Take IFA tablets regularly</strong> as advised by health worker</li>
<li><strong>Deworming</strong> — take deworming tablet every 6 months (free at Anganwadi)</li>
<li><strong>Wear footwear/slippers</strong> — prevents hookworm infection from soil</li>
<li><strong>Space pregnancies</strong> — maintain at least 3 years gap between children</li>
<li><strong>Regular health check-ups</strong> — get hemoglobin checked during pregnancy</li>
<li><strong>Personal hygiene</strong> — wash hands before eating, after toilet</li>
<li><strong>Avoid tea/coffee with meals</strong> — wait at least 1 hour</li>
</ol>

<!-- ==================== GOVERNMENT PROGRAMS ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Government Programs for Anemia Control</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB)</strong> — National program to reduce anemia prevalence by 3% per year</li>
<li><strong>Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)</strong> — Free antenatal checkups including hemoglobin test</li>
<li><strong>Weekly Iron Folic Acid Supplementation (WIFS)</strong> — For adolescent girls in schools and Anganwadis</li>
<li><strong>National Deworming Day</strong> — Albendazole tablets for all children and adolescents</li>
</ul>
