<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Introduction (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Greeting:</strong> "Good morning, respected madam and my dear mothers and sisters! My name is [Your Name] and I am a nursing student from [College Name]. Today, I am going to talk to you about a very common but serious health problem — <strong>Anemia</strong>, which many of you know as 'khoon ki kami' (blood deficiency)."</p>
<p><strong>Ice-Breaking:</strong> "How many of you sometimes feel very tired, weak, or dizzy? Raise your hands please. These could be signs of anemia. Today we will learn all about this — what causes it, what to eat, and how to prevent it!"</p>

<blockquote style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Key Message:</strong> "Healthy blood makes a healthy body — prevent anemia, stay strong!"
</blockquote>

<!-- ==================== DEFINITION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. What is Anemia? (2-3 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Simple Definition:</strong> "Anemia means your blood has become weak — it does not have enough <strong>hemoglobin</strong>. Hemoglobin is the red substance in your blood that carries oxygen to all parts of your body. When hemoglobin is low, your body does not get enough oxygen, and you feel weak and tired."</p>
<p><strong>Scientific Definition:</strong> According to WHO, hemoglobin less than <strong>11 g/dL</strong> in pregnant women and less than <strong>12 g/dL</strong> in non-pregnant women is considered anemia.</p>

<div style="background:#d4edda;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #28a745;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Normal Hemoglobin Levels:</strong><br>
• Adult Non-Pregnant Women: 12-16 g/dL<br>
• Pregnant Women: 11-14 g/dL<br>
• Adolescent Girls (12-19 years): 12-15 g/dL
</div>

<!-- ==================== MAGNITUDE ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. How Common is Anemia in India? (2 Minutes)</h3>
<ul>
<li>According to NFHS-5, <strong>57% of women</strong> in India are anemic</li>
<li><strong>52% of pregnant women</strong> suffer from anemia</li>
<li><strong>54% of adolescent girls</strong> are anemic</li>
<li>Anemia contributes to <strong>20-40% of maternal deaths</strong></li>
</ul>

<!-- ==================== ENHANCE IRON ABSORPTION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. Tips to Enhance Iron Absorption (3-4 Minutes)</h3>

<div style="background:#d4edda;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #28a745;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>✅ Do's (Increase Iron Absorption):</strong>
<ul style="margin:10px 0 0 20px;">
<li>Eat <strong>Vitamin C rich foods</strong> with meals — lemon, amla, orange, tomato</li>
<li>Cook in <strong>iron kadai</strong> — adds iron to food</li>
<li>Add <strong>sprouted grains and pulses</strong> to your diet</li>
<li>Eat <strong>jaggery after meals</strong> instead of sugar</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div style="background:#f8d7da;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #dc3545;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>❌ Don'ts (Reduce Iron Absorption):</strong>
<ul style="margin:10px 0 0 20px;">
<li>Do NOT drink <strong>tea or coffee</strong> immediately after meals — wait 1 hour</li>
<li>Avoid <strong>milk/paneer</strong> at the same time as iron-rich meals</li>
</ul>
</div>

• Stools may become black — this is <strong>normal</strong>
</blockquote>

<!-- ==================== PREVENTION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Prevention of Anemia (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<ol>
<li><strong>Eat iron-rich foods daily</strong> — green leafy vegetables, pulses, jaggery</li>
<li><strong>Take IFA tablets regularly</strong> as advised</li>
<li><strong>Deworming</strong> — take deworming tablet every 6 months</li>
<li><strong>Wear footwear</strong> — prevents hookworm infection</li>
<li><strong>Space pregnancies</strong> — 3 years gap between children</li>
<li><strong>Regular health check-ups</strong> — hemoglobin test during pregnancy</li>
<li><strong>Avoid tea/coffee with meals</strong></li>
</ol>
