<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📖 Content of Health Talk</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Introduction (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Greeting:</strong> "Good morning, respected teachers and my dear friends! My name is [Your Name] and I am a nursing student from [College Name]. Today, I am going to talk about an important nursing topic — Respiratory Assessment. Breathing is one of the most essential functions of life. If breathing is affected, the whole body can be affected because oxygen is required by every cell of our body."</p>
<p><strong>Ice-Breaking:</strong> "Have you ever seen a person breathing very fast, coughing continuously, or feeling difficulty in breathing? These signs tell us that the respiratory system may not be working properly. Today we will learn how nurses assess breathing and identify respiratory problems early."</p>

<blockquote style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Key Message:</strong> "Early respiratory assessment helps in early detection and prevention of serious breathing problems."
</blockquote>

<!-- ==================== DEFINITION ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Definition of Respiratory Assessment (2-3 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Respiratory assessment</strong> is a systematic examination of the respiratory system to evaluate breathing pattern, respiratory rate, chest movement, oxygenation status, breath sounds, cough, sputum, and signs of respiratory distress.</p>
<p>It is an important nursing assessment used to identify normal and abnormal respiratory findings and to plan appropriate nursing care.</p>

<!-- ==================== BATHING ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Normal and Abnormal Respiratory Findings</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Normal breathing:</strong> Quiet, regular, effortless breathing with respiratory rate 12-20/minute in adults</li>
<li><strong>Tachypnea:</strong> Abnormally fast breathing, commonly seen in fever, anxiety, pneumonia, or respiratory distress</li>
<li><strong>Bradypnea:</strong> Abnormally slow breathing, may occur in head injury, drug overdose, or serious illness</li>
<li><strong>Dyspnea:</strong> Difficulty in breathing or shortness of breath</li>
<li><strong>Orthopnea:</strong> Difficulty breathing while lying flat; patient feels better in sitting position</li>
<li><strong>Cyanosis:</strong> Bluish discoloration of lips, tongue, nails, or skin due to low oxygen level</li>
<li><strong>Wheezing:</strong> Musical sound during breathing, commonly seen in asthma or airway narrowing</li>
<li><strong>Crackles:</strong> Fine bubbling sounds, may be heard in pneumonia, pulmonary edema, or fluid in lungs</li>
<li><strong>Chest retractions:</strong> Inward pulling of chest wall during breathing, especially serious in children</li>
</ul>

<!-- ==================== NAIL CARE ==================== -->
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Important History Questions in Respiratory Assessment</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Cough:</strong> Ask whether cough is dry or productive and how long it has been present</li>
<li><strong>Sputum:</strong> Ask about colour, amount, smell, and presence of blood</li>
<li><strong>Breathlessness:</strong> Ask when it occurs — during walking, climbing stairs, lying down, or at rest</li>
<li><strong>Chest pain:</strong> Ask about site, nature, duration, and relation with breathing or coughing</li>
<li><strong>Fever:</strong> Fever with cough may indicate respiratory infection</li>
<li><strong>Past history:</strong> Ask about asthma, tuberculosis, COPD, pneumonia, allergy, or hospitalization</li>
<li><strong>Smoking history:</strong> Ask about smoking, tobacco use, and exposure to smoke or pollution</li>
<li><strong>Medication history:</strong> Ask about inhalers, nebulization, oxygen therapy, or current medicines</li>
</ul>
