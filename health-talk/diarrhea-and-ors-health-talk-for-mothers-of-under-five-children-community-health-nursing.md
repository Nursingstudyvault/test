<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Introduction (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Greeting:</strong> "Good morning respected madam/sir and dear mothers. My name is [Your Name], and I am a nursing student from [College Name]. Today I am going to give a health talk on diarrhea and ORS. Diarrhea is common in children, especially under five years of age, but it can become dangerous if dehydration occurs. Timely ORS, breastfeeding, feeding, and medical care can save a child’s life."</p>

<p><strong>Ice-Breaking:</strong> "How many of you have seen loose motions in children? Do you know how to prepare ORS at home using an ORS packet? Today we will learn the correct method of ORS preparation, signs of dehydration, and when to take the child to a health facility."</p>

<blockquote style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Key Message:</strong> "ORS and zinc save children from dehydration and complications of diarrhea."
</blockquote>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Definition of Diarrhea</h3>
<p><strong>Diarrhea</strong> means passing loose or watery stools three or more times in a day. It may be caused by infection, contaminated food or water, poor hygiene, or other illnesses. The main danger of diarrhea is loss of water and salts from the body, leading to dehydration.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Why Diarrhea is Dangerous?</h3>
<ul>
<li>Child loses water and salts through loose stools and vomiting.</li>
<li>Dehydration can occur quickly in infants and young children.</li>
<li>Severe dehydration may cause shock, unconsciousness, and death if not treated.</li>
<li>Repeated diarrhea can lead to malnutrition and poor growth.</li>
<li>Diarrhea with blood or persistent vomiting needs urgent medical attention.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. What is ORS?</h3>
<p><strong>ORS</strong> means Oral Rehydration Solution. It is a special solution made with clean water and ORS powder. ORS replaces water and salts lost from the body during diarrhea and helps prevent dehydration.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Correct Method of Preparing ORS</h3>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. If Child Vomits After ORS</h3>
<ul>
<li>Do not stop ORS.</li>
<li>Wait for 5-10 minutes.</li>
<li>Start again slowly with small sips using spoon.</li>
<li>Give 1-2 teaspoons every 1-2 minutes.</li>
<li>If vomiting continues repeatedly, take child to health facility.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Home Available Fluids</h3>
<p>If ORS is not immediately available, mothers can give safe home available fluids while arranging ORS or going to a health facility.</p>

<ul>
<li>Rice water / kanji</li>
<li>Dal water</li>
<li>Lemon water with salt and sugar in correct amount</li>
<li>Coconut water</li>
<li>Clean drinking water</li>
<li>Breast milk for breastfeeding child</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Continued Feeding During Diarrhea</h3>
<ul>
<li>Continue breastfeeding frequently.</li>
<li>Do not stop food during diarrhea.</li>
<li>Give soft, easily digestible foods such as rice, khichdi, dal, curd, banana, and mashed vegetables.</li>
<li>Give small frequent meals.</li>
<li>After diarrhea stops, give one extra meal daily for about 2 weeks to regain weight.</li>
<li>Avoid very oily, spicy, stale, and uncovered foods.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">12. Zinc Supplementation</h3>
<p><strong>Zinc</strong> is recommended during diarrhea because it helps reduce duration and severity of diarrhea and prevents repeated episodes. Zinc should be given as advised by doctor/ANM/health worker, commonly for 14 days.</p>

<div style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px;border-radius:8px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Teaching Point:</strong> Even if diarrhea stops earlier, complete the full zinc course as advised by health worker.
</div>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">13. What Not To Do During Diarrhea</h3>
<ul>
<li>Do not stop breastfeeding.</li>
<li>Do not stop feeding the child.</li>
<li>Do not give antibiotics or anti-diarrheal medicines without doctor’s advice.</li>
<li>Do not prepare ORS with too little or too much water.</li>
<li>Do not use ORS solution after 24 hours.</li>
<li>Do not give bottle feeding if avoidable, as it increases infection risk.</li>
<li>Do not ignore signs of dehydration.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">16. Role of Mother During Diarrhea</h3>
<ul>
<li>Start ORS as soon as diarrhea begins.</li>
<li>Continue breastfeeding and feeding.</li>
<li>Give extra fluids after every loose stool.</li>
<li>Give zinc as advised for full course.</li>
<li>Observe for dehydration and danger signs.</li>
<li>Keep child clean and prevent diaper rash.</li>
<li>Maintain hand hygiene and safe disposal of stool.</li>
<li>Take child to health facility if danger signs appear.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">17. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Health education:</strong> Teach mothers about diarrhea, ORS, zinc, feeding, and danger signs.</li>
<li><strong>Demonstration:</strong> Demonstrate correct ORS preparation using packet and clean water.</li>
<li><strong>Assessment:</strong> Assess dehydration signs and general condition of child.</li>
<li><strong>Distribution:</strong> Provide ORS packets and zinc tablets/syrup as per guidelines.</li>
<li><strong>Referral:</strong> Refer child with danger signs or severe dehydration immediately.</li>
<li><strong>Prevention:</strong> Educate family about hand washing, safe water, sanitation, breastfeeding, and immunization.</li>
<li><strong>Follow-up:</strong> Check improvement, feeding, hydration, and completion of zinc course.</li>
</ul>
