<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Introduction (3-4 Minutes)</h3>
<p><strong>Greeting:</strong> "Good morning respected madam/sir and dear participants. My name is [Your Name], and I am a nursing student from [College Name]. Today I am going to give a health talk on family planning methods. Family planning helps couples to decide when to have children, how many children to have, and how much gap should be kept between pregnancies."</p>

<p><strong>Ice-Breaking:</strong> "Many couples have questions like which method is safe, whether family planning affects health, and how much gap is good between two children. Today we will discuss these points clearly and simply."</p>

<blockquote style="background:#e8f0fe;padding:15px 20px;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;border-radius:5px;margin:15px 0;">
<strong>Key Message:</strong> "Small family, healthy family — planned pregnancy protects mother, child, and family."
</blockquote>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Definition of Family Planning</h3>
<p><strong>Family planning</strong> means the voluntary and informed decision by couples to plan the number of children, timing of pregnancy, and spacing between births by using safe and suitable contraceptive methods.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Importance of Family Planning</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Protects mother’s health:</strong> Prevents repeated and closely spaced pregnancies, reducing risk of anemia, weakness, complications, and maternal death.</li>
<li><strong>Protects child’s health:</strong> Proper spacing improves breastfeeding, nutrition, growth, and care of the child.</li>
<li><strong>Prevents unwanted pregnancy:</strong> Helps avoid unplanned pregnancy and unsafe abortion.</li>
<li><strong>Improves family welfare:</strong> Parents can provide better food, education, care, and emotional support.</li>
<li><strong>Reduces population burden:</strong> Supports community and national health development.</li>
<li><strong>Promotes responsible parenthood:</strong> Helps couples make informed and healthy reproductive choices.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Ideal Birth Spacing</h3>
<div style="background:#fff3cd;padding:15px;border-radius:8px;margin:15px 0;text-align:center;">
👶 <strong>Important Message:</strong> A gap of at least <strong>3 years</strong> between two children is beneficial for mother and child health.
</div>

<ul>
<li>Mother gets time to recover physically and mentally.</li>
<li>Baby gets proper breastfeeding, nutrition, and care.</li>
<li>Risk of low birth weight, preterm birth, and maternal weakness decreases.</li>
<li>Family can plan resources better.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Barrier Method — Condom</h3>
<p><strong>Condom</strong> is a thin protective sheath used by males during sexual intercourse. It prevents sperm from entering the female reproductive tract and also protects from sexually transmitted infections.</p>

<h4>Advantages of Condom:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Easy to use and easily available.</li>
<li>No hormonal side effects.</li>
<li>Provides protection from sexually transmitted infections including HIV.</li>
<li>Can be used whenever needed.</li>
<li>Does not affect breastfeeding.</li>
<li>No need for hospital procedure.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Important Points for Condom Use:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Use a new condom every time.</li>
<li>Check expiry date and packet damage before use.</li>
<li>Do not reuse condom.</li>
<li>Dispose used condom safely in dustbin.</li>
<li>Do not use oil-based substances with condom.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">7. Oral Contraceptive Pills</h3>
<p><strong>Oral contraceptive pills</strong> are tablets taken by women to prevent pregnancy. They work mainly by preventing ovulation and making cervical mucus thick.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">8. IUCD — Intrauterine Contraceptive Device</h3>
<p><strong>IUCD</strong> is a small device inserted into the uterus by a trained health worker or doctor to prevent pregnancy for a long duration. It is a reversible spacing method.</p>

<h4>Advantages of IUCD:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Long-acting and effective method.</li>
<li>Does not require daily attention.</li>
<li>Can be removed when couple wants pregnancy.</li>
<li>Does not interfere with sexual activity.</li>
<li>Suitable for many women after proper screening.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Possible Side Effects:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Mild lower abdominal pain after insertion.</li>
<li>Increased bleeding or cramps during periods in some women.</li>
<li>Spotting may occur initially.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Warning Signs After IUCD Insertion:</h4>
<ul>
<li>Severe lower abdominal pain.</li>
<li>Foul-smelling vaginal discharge.</li>
<li>Fever or chills.</li>
<li>Heavy bleeding.</li>
<li>Missed period or suspicion of pregnancy.</li>
<li>IUCD thread not felt or device coming out.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">9. Injectable Contraceptive</h3>
<p><strong>Injectable contraceptive</strong> is a hormonal method given by injection at regular intervals as advised by health worker. It prevents pregnancy by stopping ovulation and thickening cervical mucus.</p>

<ul>
<li>Useful for women who cannot remember daily pills.</li>
<li>Provides protection for a fixed period after each injection.</li>
<li>May cause irregular bleeding, delayed periods, or amenorrhea in some women.</li>
<li>Next injection should be taken on time.</li>
<li>Medical counselling is important before starting.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">10. Emergency Contraception</h3>
<p><strong>Emergency contraception</strong> is used to prevent pregnancy after unprotected intercourse, condom breakage, or missed contraceptive method. It should be taken as early as possible as advised by health worker.</p>

<div style="background:#fff3cd;padding:15px;border-radius:8px;margin:15px 0;text-align:center;">
⚠️ <strong>Important:</strong> Emergency contraception is not a regular family planning method. It should be used only in emergency situations.
</div>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">11. Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM)</h3>
<p><strong>LAM</strong> is a temporary natural method that may prevent pregnancy when the mother is exclusively breastfeeding, menstruation has not returned, and the baby is below 6 months of age.</p>

<ul>
<li>Baby should receive exclusive breastfeeding day and night.</li>
<li>No long gaps between feeds.</li>
<li>If menstruation returns, baby is older than 6 months, or breastfeeding is not exclusive, another method should be used.</li>
<li>Health worker counselling is necessary.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">15. Warning Signs — When to Seek Medical Help</h3>
<ul>
<li>Severe lower abdominal pain after IUCD insertion.</li>
<li>Heavy vaginal bleeding or foul-smelling discharge.</li>
<li>Fever after IUCD insertion or sterilization procedure.</li>
<li>Severe headache, chest pain, leg swelling, or visual problems while using hormonal methods.</li>
<li>Missed period or suspected pregnancy while using any method.</li>
<li>Allergic reaction, rash, or breathing difficulty after any medicine/injection.</li>
<li>Severe pain, swelling, pus, or bleeding after sterilization procedure.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">16. Role of Nurse / ANM / ASHA in Family Planning</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Health education:</strong> Explain benefits of family planning and birth spacing.</li>
<li><strong>Counselling:</strong> Help couples choose suitable method according to age, parity, health condition, and preference.</li>
<li><strong>Informed choice:</strong> Provide correct information about advantages, side effects, and alternatives.</li>
<li><strong>Distribution:</strong> Provide condoms and oral pills as per programme guidelines.</li>
<li><strong>Referral:</strong> Refer for IUCD insertion, injectable contraception, sterilization, or management of complications.</li>
<li><strong>Follow-up:</strong> Monitor side effects, missed doses, IUCD check-up, and continuation of method.</li>
<li><strong>Male involvement:</strong> Motivate husbands to participate in family planning decisions.</li>
<li><strong>Record keeping:</strong> Maintain eligible couple register and follow-up records.</li>
</ul>
