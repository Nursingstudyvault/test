<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">1. Definition</h2>
<p><strong>Respiration counting</strong> is a nursing procedure used to observe, count and record the number of breaths taken by a patient in one minute. It includes assessment of respiratory rate, rhythm, depth and character of breathing.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">2. Purposes / Objectives</h2>
<ul>
<li>To assess the respiratory rate of the patient.</li>
<li>To observe rhythm, depth and character of breathing.</li>
<li>To detect respiratory abnormalities early.</li>
<li>To monitor patient’s general condition and oxygenation status.</li>
<li>To assess response to treatment, oxygen therapy or medication.</li>
<li>To identify signs of respiratory distress.</li>
<li>To record vital signs accurately in the TPR chart.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">5. Indications</h2>
<ul>
<li>On admission of patient.</li>
<li>During routine vital signs monitoring.</li>
<li>When patient has cough, cold, fever or chest pain.</li>
<li>In patients with asthma, COPD, pneumonia or tuberculosis.</li>
<li>Before and after oxygen therapy or nebulization.</li>
<li>Before and after surgery.</li>
<li>In unconscious, critically ill or emergency patients.</li>
<li>When patient complains of breathlessness or difficulty in breathing.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">7. Preparation of Patient</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify the patient correctly.</li>
<li>Keep the patient comfortable and relaxed.</li>
<li>Position the patient in sitting or lying position.</li>
<li>Avoid telling the patient directly that respiration is being counted, if possible.</li>
<li>Ensure chest and abdomen movements can be observed without exposing the patient unnecessarily.</li>
<li>Allow rest for a few minutes if patient has recently walked, cried or exercised.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">8. Preparation of Nurse</h2>
<ul>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Keep watch/timer ready.</li>
<li>Maintain privacy and dignity of patient.</li>
<li>Observe breathing without making the patient conscious of it.</li>
<li>Know normal respiratory rate according to age.</li>
<li>Be alert for signs of respiratory distress.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">10. Abnormal Respiratory Patterns</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tachypnea:</strong> Respiratory rate above normal range.</li>
<li><strong>Bradypnea:</strong> Respiratory rate below normal range.</li>
<li><strong>Apnea:</strong> Temporary absence of breathing.</li>
<li><strong>Dyspnea:</strong> Difficulty or discomfort in breathing.</li>
<li><strong>Orthopnea:</strong> Difficulty breathing while lying flat.</li>
<li><strong>Cheyne-Stokes respiration:</strong> Gradual increase and decrease in depth of breathing followed by apnea.</li>
<li><strong>Kussmaul respiration:</strong> Deep, rapid and laboured breathing, commonly seen in metabolic acidosis.</li>
<li><strong>Biot’s respiration:</strong> Irregular breathing with periods of apnea.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">11. Signs of Respiratory Distress</h2>
<ul>
<li>Fast or very slow breathing.</li>
<li>Nasal flaring.</li>
<li>Use of accessory muscles.</li>
<li>Chest retractions.</li>
<li>Cyanosis of lips, tongue or nails.</li>
<li>Restlessness, confusion or drowsiness.</li>
<li>Wheezing, stridor or noisy breathing.</li>
<li>Inability to speak full sentences.</li>
<li>Low oxygen saturation if pulse oximeter is used.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">12. Important Points</h2>
<ul>
<li>Respiration should be counted without making the patient aware.</li>
<li>Count for one full minute in children, elderly and seriously ill patients.</li>
<li>One respiration means one inspiration and one expiration.</li>
<li>Observe rate, rhythm, depth and effort along with counting.</li>
<li>Respiration may increase due to fever, pain, anxiety, exercise and respiratory disease.</li>
<li>Report abnormal breathing immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">13. Precautions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Do not tell the patient directly that respiration is being counted if avoidable.</li>
<li>Do not count immediately after exercise, crying or emotional stress unless clinically required.</li>
<li>Maintain privacy and comfort.</li>
<li>Do not expose patient unnecessarily.</li>
<li>Count for full one minute if respiration is irregular.</li>
<li>Observe for respiratory distress while counting.</li>
<li>Record findings immediately and accurately.</li>
<li>Report abnormal findings without delay.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">14. After Care</h2>
<ul>
<li>Make the patient comfortable.</li>
<li>Maintain proper position for easy breathing.</li>
<li>Replace articles properly.</li>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Record findings in TPR/vital signs chart.</li>
<li>Report abnormal respiratory rate or distress signs to staff nurse/doctor/clinical instructor.</li>
<li>Continue monitoring as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">15. Documentation / Recording</h2>
<ul>
<li>Date and time of respiration counting.</li>
<li>Respiratory rate per minute.</li>
<li>Rhythm: regular or irregular.</li>
<li>Depth: shallow, normal or deep.</li>
<li>Effort: easy or laboured.</li>
<li>Associated symptoms: cough, chest pain, wheezing, cyanosis or breathlessness.</li>
<li>Oxygen therapy if patient is receiving.</li>
<li>Action taken and reporting if abnormal.</li>
<li>Name/signature of student nurse.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">16. Health Education</h2>
<ul>
<li>Teach patient to report breathlessness, chest pain, persistent cough or wheezing.</li>
<li>Advise patient to avoid smoking and exposure to dust or smoke.</li>
<li>Encourage deep breathing and coughing exercises if advised.</li>
<li>Teach proper use of inhaler/nebulizer if prescribed.</li>
<li>Encourage adequate fluid intake if not contraindicated.</li>
<li>Advise patient to take medicines regularly as prescribed.</li>
<li>Seek medical help if breathing becomes fast, difficult, noisy or associated with bluish lips/nails.</li>
</ul>

<p>Respiration counting is an essential vital sign procedure used to assess breathing rate and respiratory pattern. It should be performed carefully while maintaining patient comfort, privacy and accurate documentation.</p>
