<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">1. Definition</h2>
<p><strong>Blood pressure measurement</strong> is a nursing procedure used to measure the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of arteries. It is recorded as systolic pressure over diastolic pressure in millimeters of mercury (mmHg).</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">2. Purposes / Objectives</h2>
<ul>
<li>To assess the blood pressure of the patient.</li>
<li>To detect hypertension or hypotension.</li>
<li>To assess cardiovascular status.</li>
<li>To monitor changes in patient’s condition.</li>
<li>To evaluate response to antihypertensive or other medications.</li>
<li>To detect shock, bleeding, dehydration or circulatory failure early.</li>
<li>To record vital signs accurately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">4. Types of Blood Pressure</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Systolic Blood Pressure:</strong> Maximum pressure in arteries during contraction of ventricles.</li>
<li><strong>Diastolic Blood Pressure:</strong> Minimum pressure in arteries during relaxation of ventricles.</li>
<li><strong>Pulse Pressure:</strong> Difference between systolic and diastolic pressure.</li>
<li><strong>Mean Arterial Pressure:</strong> Average pressure in arteries during one cardiac cycle.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">5. Indications</h2>
<ul>
<li>On admission of patient.</li>
<li>During routine vital signs monitoring.</li>
<li>Before and after surgery.</li>
<li>Before and after administration of antihypertensive drugs.</li>
<li>When patient complains of headache, dizziness, chest pain or fainting.</li>
<li>In pregnancy, hypertension, cardiac disease, renal disease and diabetes.</li>
<li>In emergency, shock, bleeding, dehydration or unconscious patient.</li>
<li>Before blood transfusion and during monitoring as per protocol.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">7. Preparation of Patient</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify the patient correctly.</li>
<li>Explain the procedure in simple language.</li>
<li>Provide privacy and comfort.</li>
<li>Ask patient to sit or lie comfortably.</li>
<li>Allow the patient to rest for at least 5 minutes before measurement.</li>
<li>Ensure patient has not taken tea, coffee, smoked or exercised recently.</li>
<li>Support the arm at heart level.</li>
<li>Expose the upper arm without tight clothing.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">8. Preparation of Nurse</h2>
<ul>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Collect all articles.</li>
<li>Check BP apparatus is working properly.</li>
<li>Select appropriate cuff size.</li>
<li>Ensure mercury/aneroid scale is at eye level.</li>
<li>Know normal BP range and patient’s previous BP if available.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">10. Factors Affecting Blood Pressure</h2>
<ul>
<li>Age and gender.</li>
<li>Exercise and physical activity.</li>
<li>Stress, anxiety and pain.</li>
<li>Body position.</li>
<li>Smoking, caffeine and alcohol.</li>
<li>Obesity and diet high in salt.</li>
<li>Medications.</li>
<li>Cardiac, renal and endocrine diseases.</li>
<li>Pregnancy.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">11. Important Points</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use correct cuff size for accurate reading.</li>
<li>Arm should be supported at heart level.</li>
<li>Do not measure BP over thick clothing.</li>
<li>Do not talk during BP measurement.</li>
<li>Wait 1–2 minutes before repeating BP on same arm.</li>
<li>Avoid BP measurement on arm with IV line, injury, fistula, paralysis or post-mastectomy side.</li>
<li>Record the arm used and patient position if clinically important.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">12. Precautions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Check equipment before use.</li>
<li>Ensure cuff is not too loose or too tight.</li>
<li>Do not inflate cuff repeatedly without allowing rest.</li>
<li>Deflate cuff slowly and completely.</li>
<li>Maintain patient privacy and comfort.</li>
<li>Do not place stethoscope under cuff.</li>
<li>Record reading immediately and accurately.</li>
<li>Report very high or very low BP immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">13. After Care</h2>
<ul>
<li>Remove cuff and make patient comfortable.</li>
<li>Replace articles in proper place.</li>
<li>Clean stethoscope earpieces and diaphragm if required.</li>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Record BP in vital signs chart.</li>
<li>Report abnormal findings.</li>
<li>Continue monitoring as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">14. Documentation / Recording</h2>
<ul>
<li>Date and time of BP measurement.</li>
<li>BP reading in mmHg, e.g., 120/80 mmHg.</li>
<li>Arm used: right or left arm.</li>
<li>Patient position: sitting, lying or standing.</li>
<li>Any symptoms such as headache, dizziness, chest pain or fainting.</li>
<li>Medication given if any.</li>
<li>Action taken and reporting if abnormal.</li>
<li>Name/signature of student nurse.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">15. Health Education</h2>
<ul>
<li>Advise regular BP monitoring if patient has hypertension.</li>
<li>Reduce salt and oily food intake.</li>
<li>Encourage balanced diet rich in fruits and vegetables.</li>
<li>Maintain healthy body weight.</li>
<li>Encourage regular walking or exercise as advised.</li>
<li>Avoid smoking, alcohol and excessive caffeine.</li>
<li>Take antihypertensive medicines regularly as prescribed.</li>
<li>Do not stop BP medicines without medical advice.</li>
<li>Seek medical help for severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, weakness, fainting or very high BP.</li>
</ul>
