<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">1. Definition</h2>
<p><strong>Temperature recording</strong> is a nursing procedure used to measure and record the body temperature of a patient/client with the help of a thermometer in order to assess body heat, identify fever or hypothermia, and monitor the patient’s health condition.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">2. Purposes / Objectives</h2>
<ul>
<li>To assess the body temperature of the patient/client.</li>
<li>To detect fever, hypothermia, infection or inflammation.</li>
<li>To monitor changes in patient’s condition.</li>
<li>To evaluate response to treatment such as antipyretics or antibiotics.</li>
<li>To record vital signs accurately.</li>
<li>To help in diagnosis and planning nursing care.</li>
<li>To identify complications early.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">4. Indications</h2>
<ul>
<li>On admission of patient.</li>
<li>During routine vital signs monitoring.</li>
<li>When patient complains of fever, chills or weakness.</li>
<li>Before and after surgery.</li>
<li>Before and after blood transfusion.</li>
<li>During infection, inflammation or dehydration.</li>
<li>To monitor response to antipyretic medication.</li>
<li>In newborns, children, elderly and critically ill patients.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">5. Contraindications / Avoid Oral Temperature In</h2>
<ul>
<li>Unconscious or confused patient.</li>
<li>Children below 5 years.</li>
<li>Patient with oral surgery or mouth injury.</li>
<li>Patient receiving oxygen by mask.</li>
<li>Patient having seizures.</li>
<li>Patient who has taken hot or cold drinks recently.</li>
<li>Patient with severe cough or breathing difficulty.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">7. Preparation of Patient</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify the patient correctly.</li>
<li>Explain the procedure in simple language.</li>
<li>Provide privacy and comfort.</li>
<li>Ask whether the patient has taken hot/cold drink recently.</li>
<li>Ask patient not to talk during oral temperature measurement.</li>
<li>Position the patient comfortably in sitting or lying position.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">8. Preparation of Nurse</h2>
<ul>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Collect all articles.</li>
<li>Check thermometer is clean and working.</li>
<li>Check battery in digital thermometer.</li>
<li>Maintain infection control precautions.</li>
<li>Know the correct site for temperature measurement.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">10. Important Points</h2>
<ul>
<li>Wait 15–30 minutes after hot or cold drinks before oral temperature.</li>
<li>Axillary temperature is safer for children and unconscious patients.</li>
<li>Rectal temperature should be avoided in rectal surgery, diarrhea, bleeding piles and neutropenic patients.</li>
<li>Always mention the site of temperature recording.</li>
<li>Report very high or very low temperature immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">11. Precautions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Use clean and disinfected thermometer.</li>
<li>Do not use oral route in unconscious, confused or small children.</li>
<li>Do not leave patient alone with thermometer in mouth.</li>
<li>Do not force thermometer into mouth, axilla or rectum.</li>
<li>Do not record temperature immediately after exercise, bath or hot/cold drinks.</li>
<li>Handle glass thermometer carefully to prevent breakage.</li>
<li>Maintain privacy and comfort.</li>
<li>Record temperature accurately and immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">12. After Care</h2>
<ul>
<li>Make patient comfortable.</li>
<li>Clean and disinfect thermometer.</li>
<li>Replace articles in proper place.</li>
<li>Dispose waste safely.</li>
<li>Perform hand hygiene.</li>
<li>Report abnormal temperature to staff nurse/doctor/clinical instructor.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">13. Documentation / Recording</h2>
<ul>
<li>Date and time of temperature recording.</li>
<li>Temperature reading in °C or °F.</li>
<li>Site used: oral / axillary / rectal / tympanic.</li>
<li>Associated symptoms such as chills, sweating, weakness or shivering.</li>
<li>Medication given if any.</li>
<li>Signature/name of student nurse.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">14. Health Education</h2>
<ul>
<li>Teach patient to report fever, chills, sweating or weakness.</li>
<li>Encourage adequate fluid intake if not contraindicated.</li>
<li>Advise light clothing during fever.</li>
<li>Do not take medicine without medical advice.</li>
<li>Maintain personal hygiene and hand washing.</li>
<li>Follow doctor’s advice and complete prescribed medication.</li>
<li>Seek medical help if fever is high, persistent, or associated with breathing difficulty, confusion, convulsions or dehydration.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">16. Conclusion</h2>
<p>Temperature recording is an important vital sign procedure used to assess the thermal status of the body. Accurate measurement and proper documentation help in early identification of fever, hypothermia, infection and other health problems. The procedure should be performed carefully by maintaining patient comfort, safety and infection control practices.</p>
