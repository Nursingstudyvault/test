<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">History of Present Illness</h3>
<p>
Mrs. Kavita Sharma, a 42-year-old female, was admitted to the female surgical ward with complaints of right upper abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and indigestion. The pain was initially intermittent and increased after taking oily and fatty food. Gradually, the pain became more severe and radiated to the right shoulder and back.
</p>

<p>
The patient also complained of bloating, loss of appetite and mild fever. She had similar episodes of abdominal discomfort in the past after heavy meals, but the pain was relieved with medication. This time, pain was persistent and associated with vomiting, so she was brought to the hospital by her husband.
</p>

<p>
On examination, tenderness was present in the right hypochondriac region. Ultrasonography abdomen showed multiple gallstones in the gallbladder with mild gallbladder wall thickening, suggestive of symptomatic cholelithiasis. The patient was admitted, medical management was started and laparoscopic cholecystectomy was planned after pre-operative preparation.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Past Medical History</h3>
<ul>
<li>No history of diabetes mellitus.</li>
<li>No history of hypertension.</li>
<li>No history of tuberculosis, asthma or epilepsy.</li>
<li>History of recurrent indigestion and right upper abdominal discomfort after fatty meals.</li>
<li>No known drug allergy.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Past Surgical History</h3>

<li>No history of blood transfusion.</li>
<li>No history of previous hospitalization for any major illness.</li>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">
📄 Page 15 — Health Education
</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Wound Care</h3>
<ul>
<li>Keep port site wounds clean and dry.</li>
<li>Do not touch wounds with unwashed hands.</li>
<li>Do not remove dressing unless advised.</li>
<li>Observe wounds for redness, swelling, discharge, foul smell or wound opening.</li>
<li>Visit hospital for dressing and follow-up as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Medication Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take antibiotics for the full prescribed course.</li>
<li>Do not skip or stop medicines without doctor’s advice.</li>
<li>Take pain medicine only as prescribed.</li>
<li>Take gastric protection medicine before breakfast if prescribed.</li>
<li>Report allergy symptoms such as rash, itching, swelling or breathing difficulty immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Diet Advice After Cholecystectomy</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take soft and easily digestible food during early recovery.</li>
<li>Take small frequent meals instead of heavy meals.</li>
<li>Avoid oily, fried, spicy and high-fat foods for some weeks.</li>
<li>Include low-fat foods such as dal, curd, fruits, vegetables and soft chapati.</li>
<li>Drink adequate water unless restricted.</li>
<li>Gradually resume normal diet as tolerated and as advised by doctor.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Activity and Rest</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take adequate rest during recovery period.</li>
<li>Walk slowly at home to improve circulation and reduce gas discomfort.</li>
<li>Avoid heavy lifting, bending and strenuous household work until advised.</li>
<li>Support abdomen while coughing, sneezing or getting up from bed.</li>
<li>Resume normal activities only after medical advice.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Prevention of Complications</h3>
<ul>
<li>Maintain hand hygiene before touching dressing area.</li>
<li>Do deep breathing exercises as advised.</li>
<li>Avoid constipation by taking fluids and fiber-rich foods.</li>
<li>Do not ignore persistent abdominal pain, vomiting or fever.</li>
<li>Attend follow-up visits regularly.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Medical Help</h3>
<ul>
<li>Fever above 100.4°F.</li>
<li>Severe abdominal pain or increasing pain.</li>
<li>Persistent vomiting.</li>
<li>Yellow discoloration of eyes or skin.</li>
<li>Redness, swelling, pus discharge or foul smell from port site wounds.</li>
<li>Bleeding from wound site.</li>
<li>Wound opening.</li>
<li>Abdominal distension or inability to pass stool/gas.</li>
</ul>
