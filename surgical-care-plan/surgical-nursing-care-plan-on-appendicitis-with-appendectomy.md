<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">History of Present Illness</h3>
<p>
Mr. Rohit Sharma, a 24-year-old male, was apparently well 1 day before admission. He developed sudden onset abdominal pain around the umbilical region in the evening. Initially, the pain was dull and intermittent, but after a few hours it shifted to the right lower quadrant of the abdomen. The pain became sharp, continuous and aggravated by movement, coughing and walking.
</p>

<p>
The patient also complained of nausea, vomiting, anorexia and low-grade fever. He took one tablet of painkiller at home, but pain was not relieved. Due to increasing abdominal pain and tenderness, he was brought to the emergency department by his father.
</p>

<p>
On examination, tenderness and guarding were present in the right iliac fossa. Rebound tenderness was positive. Blood investigation showed raised total leukocyte count. Ultrasonography abdomen showed inflamed, non-compressible appendix suggestive of acute appendicitis. The patient was admitted to the surgical ward and prepared for emergency appendectomy.
</p>

<li>No known drug allergy.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Past Surgical History</h3>

</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Summary of Hospital Stay</h3>
<p>
Mr. Rohit Sharma, a 24-year-old male, was admitted to the surgical ward with complaints of abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting, anorexia and low-grade fever. The abdominal pain initially started around the umbilical region and later shifted to the right lower quadrant. On examination, tenderness and guarding were present in the right iliac fossa with positive rebound tenderness.
</p>

<p>
Blood investigation showed raised total leukocyte count and neutrophilia. Ultrasonography abdomen showed inflamed non-compressible appendix suggestive of acute appendicitis. The patient was kept NPO, IV fluids and antibiotics were started, and emergency appendectomy was planned after pre-operative preparation and informed consent.
</p>

<p>
Open appendectomy was performed on 10/05/2026 under general anesthesia. The inflamed appendix was removed, wound was closed in layers and sterile dressing was applied. Post-operatively, the patient was monitored for pain, bleeding, infection, bowel activity and vital signs. The patient’s condition improved gradually. Pain reduced, fever subsided and wound remained clean and dry.
</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">
📄 Page 15 — Health Education
</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Wound Care</h3>
<ul>
<li>Keep the surgical wound clean and dry.</li>
<li>Do not touch the wound with unwashed hands.</li>
<li>Do not remove dressing unless advised by doctor or nurse.</li>
<li>Observe the wound daily for redness, swelling, discharge, foul smell or wound opening.</li>
<li>Visit hospital for dressing and suture removal as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Medication Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take antibiotics for the full prescribed course.</li>
<li>Do not skip or stop medicines without doctor’s advice.</li>
<li>Take pain medicine only as prescribed.</li>
<li>Take gastric protection medicine before breakfast if prescribed.</li>
<li>Report allergy symptoms such as rash, itching, swelling or breathing difficulty immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Diet Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Start with soft and easily digestible food after surgery.</li>
<li>Take small frequent meals instead of heavy meals.</li>
<li>Include protein-rich foods such as dal, milk, curd, paneer, egg or pulses to promote wound healing.</li>
<li>Drink adequate water unless restricted.</li>
<li>Avoid oily, spicy, fried and junk foods during recovery.</li>
<li>Include fruits and vegetables to prevent constipation.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Activity and Rest</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take adequate rest during the recovery period.</li>
<li>Walk slowly at home to improve circulation and prevent complications.</li>
<li>Avoid heavy lifting, running, cycling and strenuous exercise until advised by doctor.</li>
<li>Support the incision site while coughing, sneezing or getting up from bed.</li>
<li>Return to college/work only after medical advice.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Prevention of Constipation</h3>
<ul>
<li>Drink adequate fluids.</li>
<li>Take fiber-rich foods such as fruits, vegetables and whole grains.</li>
<li>Walk regularly as tolerated.</li>
<li>Do not strain during defecation.</li>
<li>Inform doctor if constipation persists.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Medical Help</h3>
<ul>
<li>Fever above 100.4°F.</li>
<li>Severe abdominal pain or increasing pain.</li>
<li>Persistent vomiting.</li>
<li>Redness, swelling, pus discharge or foul smell from wound.</li>
<li>Bleeding from wound site.</li>
<li>Wound opening or suture breakage.</li>
<li>Abdominal distension or inability to pass stool/gas.</li>
</ul>
