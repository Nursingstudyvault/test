<li>Swelling increases on coughing, standing and lifting heavy weight.</li>
<li>Swelling reduces on lying down.</li>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">History of Present Illness</h3>
<p>
Mr. Ramesh Choudhary, a 48-year-old male, was admitted to the male surgical ward with complaints of swelling in the right groin region for 6 months. Initially, the swelling was small and appeared only during coughing, standing for long time and lifting heavy objects. It disappeared on lying down.
</p>

<p>
Gradually, the swelling increased in size and was associated with dull aching pain and dragging sensation. The patient works as a farmer/labour worker and frequently lifts heavy loads, which increased the swelling and discomfort. There was no history of vomiting, abdominal distension, constipation with obstruction, fever or irreducible swelling.
</p>

<p>
On physical examination, a reducible swelling was present in the right inguinal region with positive cough impulse. The patient was diagnosed with right inguinal hernia. After routine investigations and pre-operative preparation, open mesh hernioplasty was planned.
</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Past Medical History</h3>
<ul>
<li>No history of diabetes mellitus.</li>
<li>No history of hypertension.</li>
<li>No history of tuberculosis, asthma or epilepsy.</li>
<li>History of chronic cough occasionally during winter season.</li>
<li>No history of chronic urinary obstruction.</li>
<li>No known drug allergy.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Past Surgical History</h3>

<li>No history of blood transfusion.</li>
<li>No previous hospitalization for hernia or abdominal surgery.</li>

Mr. Ramesh Choudhary, a 48-year-old male, was admitted with complaints of swelling in the right groin region for 6 months, which increased on coughing, standing and lifting heavy weight and reduced on lying down. He also complained of dull aching pain and dragging sensation in the right groin region.

On examination, a reducible swelling was present in the right inguinal region with positive cough impulse. Routine investigations were within normal limits. The patient was diagnosed with right inguinal hernia and open mesh hernioplasty was planned after pre-operative preparation.

Open mesh hernioplasty was performed on 20/05/2026 under spinal anesthesia. Post-operatively, the patient was monitored for pain, bleeding, infection, urinary retention, return of lower limb sensation after spinal anesthesia and vital signs. Patient’s condition improved gradually. Pain reduced, oral diet was tolerated and surgical dressing remained clean and dry.

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">
📄 Page 15 — Health Education
</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">1. Wound Care</h3>
<ul>
<li>Keep surgical wound clean and dry.</li>
<li>Do not touch wound with unwashed hands.</li>
<li>Do not remove dressing unless advised.</li>
<li>Observe wound for redness, swelling, discharge, foul smell or wound opening.</li>
<li>Visit hospital for dressing and follow-up as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">2. Medication Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take antibiotics for the full prescribed course.</li>
<li>Do not skip or stop medicines without doctor’s advice.</li>
<li>Take pain medicine only as prescribed.</li>
<li>Take gastric protection medicine before breakfast if prescribed.</li>
<li>Use stool softener only as advised if constipation occurs.</li>
<li>Report allergy symptoms such as rash, itching, swelling or breathing difficulty immediately.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">3. Diet Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take a balanced diet rich in protein for wound healing.</li>
<li>Include dal, milk, curd, eggs if allowed, pulses, green vegetables and fruits.</li>
<li>Drink adequate water unless restricted.</li>
<li>Take fiber-rich foods to prevent constipation.</li>
<li>Avoid excessive spicy and oily foods during early recovery.</li>
<li>Avoid overeating if it causes abdominal discomfort.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4. Activity and Rest</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take adequate rest during recovery period.</li>
<li>Walk slowly at home as tolerated.</li>
<li>Avoid heavy lifting, pushing, pulling and strenuous work until doctor permits.</li>
<li>Avoid sudden bending and excessive stair climbing during early recovery.</li>
<li>Support surgical site while coughing, sneezing or getting up from bed.</li>
<li>Resume farming/labour work only after medical advice.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">5. Prevention of Recurrence</h3>
<ul>
<li>Avoid lifting heavy weight without medical permission.</li>
<li>Treat chronic cough promptly.</li>
<li>Avoid constipation and straining during stool passage.</li>
<li>Maintain healthy body weight.</li>
<li>Use proper body mechanics while lifting after complete recovery.</li>
<li>Do regular follow-up as advised.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">6. Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Medical Help</h3>
<ul>
<li>Fever above 100.4°F.</li>
<li>Severe pain or increasing swelling in operated area.</li>
<li>Redness, swelling, pus discharge or foul smell from wound.</li>
<li>Bleeding from wound site.</li>
<li>Wound opening.</li>
<li>Difficulty in passing urine.</li>
<li>Persistent vomiting or abdominal distension.</li>
<li>New bulge or swelling in groin region.</li>
</ul>
