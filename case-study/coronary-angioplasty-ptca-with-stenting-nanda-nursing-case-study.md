<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">4. History Collection</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">4.1 History of Present Illness</h3>
<p>The patient, Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old male bank manager with a sedentary lifestyle, presented with a 2-week history of progressive exertional chest pain — retrosternal heaviness radiating to the left arm, occurring during walking, climbing stairs, and stressful work situations. Each episode lasts 5-10 minutes, relieved by rest and sublingual nitrates. Over the past week, his exercise tolerance decreased significantly — from walking 1 km comfortably to developing dyspnea after 200 meters (NYHA Class II).</p>
<p>An ECG showed ST-segment depression in anterior leads (V2-V6). 2D Echocardiogram revealed mildly reduced LVEF (52%) with anterior wall hypokinesia. Coronary Angiography via right radial approach revealed <strong>90% tubular, eccentric stenosis in the proximal LAD artery</strong>. PTCA with DES deployment was successfully performed. The patient is currently in CCU for post-procedural monitoring.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">13. Conclusion</h2>
<p>Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old male with multiple cardiovascular risk factors, presented with progressive exertional angina and was diagnosed with critical single vessel CAD — 90% LAD stenosis. He successfully underwent PTCA with DES deployment via right radial approach, achieving TIMI 3 flow and resolution of ischemic ECG changes.</p>
<p>This case study presents a comprehensive NANDA-I formatted nursing care approach addressing the complex, multi-dimensional needs of a post-angioplasty patient. Eight priority nursing diagnoses were identified, and three detailed nursing care plans were developed focusing on cardiac perfusion, bleeding risk, and patient education. The patient demonstrated an uncomplicated post-procedural course — no chest pain, normalized ECG, patent radial artery without complications, preserved renal function, and successful transition from anxious uncertainty to informed confidence.</p>
<p>This case study underscores that nursing care for the angioplasty patient extends far beyond procedural monitoring. The cardiac nurse serves as vigilant clinician, educator, counselor, and care coordinator. The critical importance of DAPT adherence cannot be overstated — premature discontinuation remains the single greatest risk factor for stent thrombosis, a catastrophic event with 20-40% mortality. Holistic, patient-centered nursing care is essential for successful recovery and secondary prevention.</p>
