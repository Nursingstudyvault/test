<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS</h3>
<p>Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old bank manager with a sedentary, high-stress lifestyle, presented with a 2-week history of progressive exertional angina. The pain was retrosternal heaviness radiating to the left arm, occurring during walking, climbing stairs, and stressful work situations. Each episode lasted 5-10 minutes, relieved by rest and sublingual Sorbitrate 5mg. Over the past week, exercise tolerance decreased from walking 1 km to developing dyspnea after only 200 meters (NYHA Class II). ECG at a local clinic showed ST depression in V2-V6, suggestive of anterior wall ischemia. 2D Echocardiogram revealed LVEF 52% with anterior wall hypokinesia. Coronary angiography via right radial approach on 11/05/2026 revealed <strong>90% tubular, eccentric stenosis in the proximal LAD artery</strong>. RCA and LCx showed only mild disease (20-30%). Same-sitting ad-hoc PTCA with Drug-Eluting Stent (DES — Sirolimus-eluting, 3.0 × 23 mm) was successfully deployed, achieving TIMI 3 flow. The patient was transferred to CCU for post-procedural monitoring.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 10 — Disease Introduction, Etiology & Pathophysiology</h2>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">INTRODUCTION</h3>
<p>Coronary Artery Disease is the narrowing/blockage of coronary arteries due to atherosclerosis, leading to myocardial ischemia. PTCA with stenting is a minimally invasive revascularization procedure to restore blood flow.</p>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">ETIOLOGY</h3><p>Atherosclerosis (plaque buildup) is the primary cause. Risk factors: HTN, DM, dyslipidemia, smoking, obesity, physical inactivity, stress, family history, age, male gender.</p>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">PATHOPHYSIOLOGY</h3><p>Endothelial injury → LDL infiltration → oxidation → macrophage foam cells → fatty streaks → smooth muscle proliferation → fibrous cap → mature plaque → stenosis → ischemia/angina. PTCA: Balloon inflation compresses plaque against vessel wall; DES deployed to maintain patency and elute drug (Sirolimus) to inhibit smooth muscle proliferation and prevent restenosis.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 11 — Clinical Manifestations, Diagnostic & Medical Management</h2>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS</h3><ul><li>Exertional angina — retrosternal heaviness radiating to left arm; dyspnea NYHA II; palpitations; diaphoresis; fatigue; ST depression on ECG.</li></ul>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">DIAGNOSTIC EVALUATION</h3><ul><li>ECG: ST depression V2-V6; Echo: LVEF 52%, anterior hypokinesia; Angiography: 90% LAD stenosis; Troponin I: 18 ng/L (normal); LDL: 142 mg/dL; HbA1c: 7.8%.</li></ul>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">MEDICAL MANAGEMENT</h3><ul><li>DAPT (Aspirin + Clopidogrel); High-intensity statin (Atorvastatin 40mg); Beta-blocker (Metoprolol); ARB (Telmisartan); Metformin; PTCA + DES; IV fluids for CIN prophylaxis; Cardiac rehabilitation.</li></ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 12 — Nursing Management, Health Education, Prognosis & Conclusion</h2>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">NURSING MANAGEMENT</h3><ul><li>Continuous cardiac monitoring; assess for chest pain; VS per PCI protocol; administer DAPT strictly on time; assess radial access site (bleeding, hematoma, neurovascular status); maintain patent hemostasis; monitor urine output; calm environment; educate about DAPT, diet, activity, follow-up.</li></ul>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">HEALTH EDUCATION</h3><ul><li>NEVER STOP DAPT without cardiologist approval; cardiac diet (low fat, low salt); complete smoking cessation; regular walking; cardiac rehab Phase II; follow-up: cardiologist 2 weeks, lipid profile 4-6 weeks, Echo 3 months, TMT 6 months.</li></ul>
<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">PROGNOSIS & CONCLUSION</h3><p>DES patency &gt;90% at 1 year. With DAPT adherence + risk factor control, excellent prognosis. Nursing care is critical for preventing stent thrombosis, ensuring recovery, and empowering lifelong heart-healthy living.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 17 — Discharge Summary</h2>
<p>Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, 58-year-old male, admitted on 10/05/2026 with exertional angina, diagnosed with single vessel CAD — 90% proximal LAD stenosis. Successful PTCA + DES (Sirolimus-eluting, 3.0×23mm) via right radial approach with TIMI 3 flow. Uncomplicated hospital course — no chest pain, no bleeding, no CIN. ECG normalized. Discharged on POD 3 in stable condition on DAPT, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker, ARB, Metformin.</p>
<p style="font-weight:600;">At discharge, the patient is advised to:</p>
<ul><li>NEVER STOP Aspirin or Clopidogrel without cardiologist approval — premature DAPT cessation = #1 cause of stent thrombosis (20-40% mortality)</li>
<li>Take all medications exactly as prescribed at the same time daily — use pill box organizer and phone alarms</li>
<li>Keep medication card in wallet listing all drugs, doses, and timings</li>
<li>Follow cardiac diet — low saturated fat, low salt (&lt;2g/day), Mediterranean diet</li>
<li>Complete smoking cessation — ZERO tobacco; avoid passive smoking</li>
<li>Avoid alcohol or limit to occasional 1 drink</li>
<li>Start with 5-10 minute walks, 2-3 times daily — gradually increase</li>
<li>No heavy lifting (&gt;5 kg) with right arm for 1 week; no driving for 48 hours</li>
<li>Attend cardiac rehabilitation Phase II program</li>
<li>Sexual activity can resume after 1-2 weeks if asymptomatic</li>
<li>Keep follow-up: Cardiologist at 2 weeks; Lipid profile + HbA1c at 4-6 weeks; Echo at 3 months; TMT at 6 months; Annual check-up lifelong</li>
<li>Report IMMEDIATELY to ER (call 108) if: Chest pain at rest; sudden shortness of breath; black/tarry stools or blood in vomit/urine; sudden severe headache with weakness/numbness; rapidly expanding swelling at right wrist; fever &gt;100.4°F with chills</li></ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 18 — Health Education</h2>
<div style="margin:15px 0;">
<p><strong>Medication Compliance (MOST CRITICAL) —</strong> Patient was emphatically educated that NEVER STOPPING Aspirin or Clopidogrel is the single most important action to prevent stent thrombosis. Explained that stopping these "blood thinners" prematurely carries a 20-40% risk of sudden, catastrophic stent blockage and death. Aspirin 150mg OD lifelong; Clopidogrel 75mg OD minimum 12 months. Must NEVER discontinue without explicit cardiologist consultation — even if another doctor/dentist suggests it. Use weekly pill box; set phone alarms; keep updated medication card in wallet.</p>
<p><strong>Dietary Advice —</strong> DO's: Fruits, vegetables, whole grains, fish (2-3×/week), egg whites, low-fat milk, nuts, olive/mustard oil. DON'Ts: Fried foods, ghee/butter, red meat, organ meats, processed foods, bakery items, excess salt (&lt;2g/day — remove salt shaker), pickles, papad, sugary drinks/sweets.</p>
<p><strong>Lifestyle Modification —</strong> Complete smoking cessation — ZERO tobacco. Alcohol — maximum 1 drink occasionally. 7-8 hours sleep. Stress management — yoga, meditation, deep breathing, regular walking.</p>
<p><strong>Exercise and Activity —</strong> Start walking 5-10 minutes, 2-3 times daily. Gradually increase by 5 min/week. Attend cardiac rehab Phase II — supervised exercise, education, counseling. Avoid heavy lifting (&gt;5 kg) with right arm ×1 week.</p>
<p><strong>Disease Awareness —</strong> Explained CAD and stent function. DES patency &gt;90% at 1 year with proper care. Stressed that DAPT non-compliance = #1 cause of stent thrombosis. Advised to recognize warning signs of stent thrombosis, bleeding, and stroke.</p>
<p><strong>Follow-up Care —</strong> Cardiologist: 2 weeks. Lipid profile + HbA1c: 4-6 weeks. Echo: 3 months. TMT: 6 months. Annual comprehensive cardiac check-up lifelong. Dental check-up important. Report immediately: Chest pain at rest, black stools, severe headache, wrist swelling, fever with chills.</p>
