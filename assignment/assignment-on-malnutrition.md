<h2>2. Introduction</h2>
<p>
Malnutrition is a serious public health problem that occurs when the body does not receive adequate nutrients required for growth, development, immunity and normal body functions. It may occur due to deficiency, excess or imbalance of energy, protein, vitamins and minerals. Malnutrition commonly affects infants, children, adolescent girls, pregnant women, lactating mothers, elderly people and people suffering from chronic diseases.
</p>

<p>
In children, malnutrition can lead to poor growth, low body weight, repeated infections, delayed development and poor school performance. In adults, it may cause weakness, reduced work capacity, poor wound healing and increased risk of illness. Nurses play an important role in identifying malnutrition, providing nutritional counselling, monitoring growth, educating families and referring severe cases for treatment.
</p>

<h2>3. Definition</h2>
<p>
Malnutrition is a condition that results from deficiency, excess or imbalance of nutrients in the diet, leading to adverse effects on body form, body function and clinical outcome.
</p>

<h2>4. Types of Malnutrition</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Undernutrition:</strong> Deficiency of energy and nutrients causing underweight, wasting and stunting.</li>
<li><strong>Overnutrition:</strong> Excess intake of calories leading to overweight and obesity.</li>
<li><strong>Micronutrient Deficiency:</strong> Deficiency of vitamins and minerals such as iron, iodine, vitamin A and vitamin D.</li>
<li><strong>Protein Energy Malnutrition:</strong> Deficiency of protein and calories, commonly seen in children.</li>
</ul>

<h2>5. Causes of Malnutrition</h2>
<ul>
<li>Poverty and low socio-economic status.</li>
<li>Inadequate intake of balanced diet.</li>
<li>Lack of knowledge about nutrition.</li>
<li>Improper breastfeeding and weaning practices.</li>
<li>Repeated infections such as diarrhea, pneumonia and worm infestation.</li>
<li>Poor sanitation and unsafe drinking water.</li>
<li>Large family size and food insecurity.</li>
<li>Chronic diseases causing poor appetite or nutrient loss.</li>
<li>Neglect of child feeding and care.</li>
<li>Food taboos and faulty food habits.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Risk Factors</h2>
<ul>
<li>Infants and under-five children</li>
<li>Low birth weight babies</li>
<li>Children with recurrent diarrhea or respiratory infection</li>
<li>Children not exclusively breastfed</li>
<li>Delayed or improper complementary feeding</li>
<li>Pregnant and lactating mothers</li>
<li>Elderly persons</li>
<li>People with chronic illness</li>
<li>Poor hygiene and sanitation</li>
<li>Low literacy level of parents</li>
</ul>

<h2>8. Protein Energy Malnutrition</h2>
<p>
Protein Energy Malnutrition is a form of undernutrition caused by inadequate intake of protein and calories. It is commonly seen among infants and young children. The two severe forms of protein energy malnutrition are marasmus and kwashiorkor.
</p>

<h2>9. Signs and Symptoms</h2>
<ul>
<li>Low body weight</li>
<li>Thin and wasted appearance</li>
<li>Failure to gain weight</li>
<li>Stunted growth</li>
<li>Weakness and tiredness</li>
<li>Poor appetite</li>
<li>Dry skin and hair changes</li>
<li>Pallor due to anemia</li>
<li>Frequent infections</li>
<li>Delayed wound healing</li>
<li>Irritability and lethargy</li>
<li>Swelling of feet or face in kwashiorkor</li>
<li>Delayed milestones in children</li>
</ul>

<h2>10. Diagnostic Evaluation</h2>
<ul>
<li>Assessment of height and weight.</li>
<li>Body Mass Index calculation in adults.</li>
<li>Mid Upper Arm Circumference measurement in children.</li>
<li>Growth chart monitoring.</li>
<li>Dietary history and 24-hour diet recall.</li>
<li>Clinical examination for wasting, edema, pallor and vitamin deficiency signs.</li>
<li>Hemoglobin estimation.</li>
<li>Serum protein and albumin level if required.</li>
<li>Stool examination for worm infestation.</li>
<li>Assessment of associated infections.</li>
</ul>

<h2>11. Management / Treatment</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify and treat the underlying cause.</li>
<li>Provide adequate calories and protein according to age and condition.</li>
<li>Correct dehydration and electrolyte imbalance if present.</li>
<li>Treat infections such as diarrhea, pneumonia and worm infestation.</li>
<li>Provide vitamin and mineral supplementation.</li>
<li>Give iron supplementation after stabilization if anemia is present.</li>
<li>Provide frequent small feeds.</li>
<li>Promote exclusive breastfeeding for infants below 6 months.</li>
<li>Start appropriate complementary feeding after 6 months.</li>
<li>Refer severe acute malnutrition cases to Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre.</li>
</ul>

<h2>12. Nursing Management</h2>
<ul>
<li>Assess nutritional status of the patient.</li>
<li>Record weight, height and MUAC regularly.</li>
<li>Monitor vital signs and hydration status.</li>
<li>Assess dietary pattern and feeding practices.</li>
<li>Provide small frequent nutritious meals.</li>
<li>Maintain warmth and prevent hypothermia in severely malnourished children.</li>
<li>Maintain hygiene and prevent infection.</li>
<li>Administer medicines and supplements as prescribed.</li>
<li>Educate mother regarding balanced diet and child feeding.</li>
<li>Encourage immunization and regular follow-up.</li>
<li>Refer severe cases to higher health facility.</li>
</ul>

<h2>14. Diet Plan for Malnutrition</h2>
<p>
A malnourished patient requires a balanced diet rich in calories, protein, vitamins and minerals. Locally available foods should be used so that the family can follow the diet easily at home.
</p>

<h2>15. Prevention of Malnutrition</h2>
<ul>
<li>Promote exclusive breastfeeding for first 6 months.</li>
<li>Start complementary feeding after 6 months with continued breastfeeding.</li>
<li>Provide balanced diet according to age.</li>
<li>Give small frequent meals to children.</li>
<li>Maintain personal hygiene and food hygiene.</li>
<li>Use safe drinking water.</li>
<li>Prevent and treat diarrhea and infections early.</li>
<li>Ensure complete immunization.</li>
<li>Provide vitamin A and iron supplementation as advised.</li>
<li>Monitor growth regularly using growth chart.</li>
<li>Educate mothers about locally available nutritious foods.</li>
</ul>

<h2>16. Health Education</h2>
<ul>
<li>Explain importance of balanced diet.</li>
<li>Teach mother about exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months.</li>
<li>Explain correct complementary feeding after 6 months.</li>
<li>Advise adding oil/ghee to child food to increase calories.</li>
<li>Encourage use of dal, pulses, milk, egg and green vegetables.</li>
<li>Teach hand washing before cooking and feeding.</li>
<li>Advise safe storage of food and drinking water.</li>
<li>Encourage regular growth monitoring at Anganwadi or health centre.</li>
<li>Advise complete immunization.</li>
<li>Teach danger signs like poor feeding, severe weakness, edema, fever and diarrhea.</li>
<li>Advise early treatment of infections.</li>
</ul>

<h2>17. Complications</h2>
<ul>
<li>Repeated infections</li>
<li>Severe weakness</li>
<li>Growth failure</li>
<li>Delayed development</li>
<li>Anemia</li>
<li>Vitamin deficiency disorders</li>
<li>Hypothermia</li>
<li>Dehydration</li>
<li>Poor wound healing</li>
<li>Increased risk of death in severe cases</li>
</ul>

<h2>18. Role of Nurse</h2>
<ul>
<li>Identify malnourished children and high-risk families.</li>
<li>Monitor growth using growth chart.</li>
<li>Measure weight, height and MUAC.</li>
<li>Provide nutrition education to mother and family.</li>
<li>Promote breastfeeding and complementary feeding.</li>
<li>Encourage immunization and vitamin supplementation.</li>
<li>Detect danger signs and refer severe cases.</li>
<li>Maintain records and follow-up.</li>
<li>Coordinate with Anganwadi worker and community health worker.</li>
<li>Conduct health education sessions in the community.</li>
</ul>

<h2>19. Conclusion</h2>
<p>
Malnutrition is a preventable and treatable condition. It affects growth, immunity, development and overall health. Early identification, proper nutrition, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, infection control, immunization, growth monitoring and health education are essential for prevention and management of malnutrition. Nurses have an important role in community awareness, nutritional counselling, early referral and follow-up care.
</p>
