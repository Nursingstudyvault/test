<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">CHIEF COMPLAINTS</h3>
<p>The patient was admitted to the medical ward with the following complaints:</p>

<ul>
<li>Severe headache since 2 days.</li>
<li>Dizziness and heaviness in head since 1 day.</li>
<li>Occasional blurring of vision.</li>
<li>Palpitations on exertion.</li>
<li>Fatigue and disturbed sleep.</li>
<li>Blood pressure recorded at home: 180/110 mmHg.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS</h3>

<p>Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old male shopkeeper, was apparently well until 2 days before admission when he developed severe headache mainly in the occipital region. The headache was dull aching in nature, persistent, and associated with heaviness of head.</p>

<p>He also complained of dizziness while standing and walking, occasional blurring of vision, and palpitations during mild exertion. The symptoms gradually increased, and the patient visited a nearby clinic where his blood pressure was recorded as 180/110 mmHg.</p>

<p>He was advised hospital admission for further evaluation and management of uncontrolled hypertension. On admission, his blood pressure was 176/108 mmHg, pulse 96/min, respiratory rate 20/min, temperature 98.6°F, and SpO₂ 98% on room air.</p>

<p>The patient gave a history of hypertension for the last 6 years but admitted irregular intake of antihypertensive medicines. He often stopped medicines when symptoms improved. He also reported high salt intake, frequent consumption of fried and oily foods, lack of regular exercise, and increased work-related stress.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">PAST MEDICAL HISTORY</h3>
<ul>
<li>Known case of hypertension for 6 years.</li>
<li>Irregular medication intake for hypertension.</li>
<li>No known history of diabetes mellitus.</li>
<li>No history of myocardial infarction, stroke, chronic kidney disease, thyroid disorder, or epilepsy.</li>
<li>No history of asthma or tuberculosis.</li>
<li>No known drug or food allergy.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">PAST SURGICAL HISTORY</h3>
<ul>
<li>No history of any major surgical intervention.</li>
<li>No history of previous hospitalization for surgery.</li>
<li>No history of blood transfusion.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">MEDICATION HISTORY</h3>
<ul>
<li>Tablet Amlodipine 5 mg once daily — irregular intake.</li>
<li>Occasional use of painkiller for headache without medical advice.</li>
<li>No regular follow-up with physician.</li>
</ul>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 15 — Discharge Summary</h2>

<p>Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old male, was admitted with complaints of severe headache, dizziness, fatigue, and elevated blood pressure. On admission, his blood pressure was 176/108 mmHg, indicating uncontrolled hypertension. He had a history of irregular medication intake, high salt consumption, sedentary lifestyle, and dyslipidemia.</p>

<p>During hospitalization, the patient was managed with prescribed antihypertensive medicines, low-salt diet, rest, regular blood pressure monitoring, and health education. Diagnostic investigations revealed elevated total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, triglycerides, and trace protein in urine, indicating the need for regular monitoring and lifestyle modification.</p>

<p>The patient’s blood pressure gradually improved from 176/108 mmHg to 132/84 mmHg. Headache and dizziness reduced, and the patient became clinically stable. He was educated regarding medication compliance, low-salt diet, regular exercise, BP monitoring, avoidance of smoking and alcohol, stress management, and regular follow-up.</p>

<div style="background:#e8f0fe;border-left:4px solid #1a73e8;padding:15px 20px;margin:15px 0;border-radius:8px;">
<strong>Condition at Discharge:</strong> Stable, conscious, oriented, headache relieved, BP controlled, no chest pain, no breathlessness, and no edema.
</div>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Discharge Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Take antihypertensive medicines regularly as prescribed.</li>
<li>Do not stop medicine without doctor’s advice even if BP becomes normal.</li>
<li>Monitor blood pressure at home and maintain a BP record.</li>
<li>Follow low-salt diet and avoid pickle, papad, namkeen, chips, and packaged foods.</li>
<li>Avoid fried, oily, and high-fat foods.</li>
<li>Walk for 30 minutes daily as tolerated.</li>
<li>Maintain healthy body weight.</li>
<li>Avoid smoking, tobacco, and alcohol.</li>
<li>Practice relaxation techniques such as deep breathing and meditation.</li>
<li>Attend regular follow-up visits.</li>
<li>Immediately report to hospital if severe headache, chest pain, breathlessness, weakness of one side of body, blurred vision, or confusion occurs.</li>
</ul>

<!-- ========== PAGE 16 ========== -->
<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 16 — Health Education</h2>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Medication Compliance</h3>
<p>The patient was educated to take all prescribed antihypertensive medicines regularly at the same time every day. He was instructed not to skip doses and not to stop medicines suddenly without consulting the physician, because uncontrolled blood pressure can lead to stroke, heart attack, kidney disease, and eye problems.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Dietary Advice</h3>
<ul>
<li>Follow a low-salt diet.</li>
<li>Limit total salt intake to about one teaspoon per day or as advised by the doctor.</li>
<li>Avoid pickle, papad, namkeen, chips, packaged snacks, processed foods, and extra salt on salad.</li>
<li>Take more fruits, vegetables, whole grains, pulses, and low-fat milk products.</li>
<li>Avoid fried foods, ghee, butter, vanaspati, fast food, and bakery items.</li>
<li>Drink adequate water unless restricted.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Lifestyle Modification</h3>
<ul>
<li>Do regular walking or light exercise for 30 minutes daily as tolerated.</li>
<li>Maintain healthy body weight.</li>
<li>Avoid smoking and tobacco completely.</li>
<li>Avoid alcohol.</li>
<li>Take adequate sleep for 7–8 hours daily.</li>
<li>Reduce stress by meditation, yoga, deep breathing, and family support.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Home Blood Pressure Monitoring</h3>
<p>The patient was advised to check blood pressure regularly at home, preferably at the same time each day. He should sit quietly for 5 minutes before checking BP, keep the arm at heart level, and record readings in a notebook to show during follow-up visits.</p>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Warning Signs</h3>
<p>The patient and family were educated to seek immediate medical help if any of the following symptoms occur:</p>
<ul>
<li>Severe headache not relieved by rest or medicine</li>
<li>Chest pain or tightness</li>
<li>Shortness of breath</li>
<li>Sudden weakness or numbness of face, arm, or leg</li>
<li>Difficulty speaking</li>
<li>Blurred vision</li>
<li>Confusion or fainting</li>
<li>Swelling of feet or reduced urine output</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="color:#1a73e8;">Follow-up Care</h3>
<p>The patient was advised to attend regular follow-up visits for blood pressure monitoring, medication adjustment, lipid profile, kidney function tests, urine examination, and assessment of complications. Family members were encouraged to support the patient in diet control, exercise, and regular medication intake.</p>

<h2 style="color:#1557b0;border-left:5px solid #1a73e8;padding-left:12px;margin:30px 0 15px;">📄 Page 17 — Conclusion</h2>

<p>This case study presents Mr. Rajendra Prasad Sharma, a 58-year-old male diagnosed with uncontrolled hypertension. The patient was admitted with complaints of headache, dizziness, fatigue, and elevated blood pressure. His condition was associated with irregular medication intake, high salt consumption, sedentary lifestyle, and dyslipidemia.</p>

<p>During hospitalization, the patient received antihypertensive medication, regular blood pressure monitoring, dietary modification, rest, and nursing care. His blood pressure gradually improved from 176/108 mmHg to 132/84 mmHg, and symptoms such as headache and dizziness were relieved.</p>

<p>The nursing care focused on assessment of vital signs, prevention of complications, medication compliance, lifestyle modification, dietary counseling, patient education, and follow-up care. The patient and family were educated about the importance of regular medication, low-salt diet, exercise, stress management, and early recognition of warning signs.</p>

<p>Hypertension is a chronic condition that requires lifelong management. With proper medication adherence, lifestyle modification, regular follow-up, and family support, complications such as stroke, myocardial infarction, renal failure, and heart failure can be prevented. This case study highlights the important role of nurses in health education, monitoring, prevention of complications, and promotion of self-care among hypertensive patients.</p>
